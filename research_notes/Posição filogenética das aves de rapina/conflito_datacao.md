# Phylogenomic conflict, divergence times and the definition of "raptor" in Telluraves

Scope: why the raptor-bearing nodes of Telluraves (core landbirds) are unstable; dated estimates for raptor nodes across studies; biogeographic hypotheses; the modern definition of "raptor/bird of prey". State of evidence as of October 2026. Bibliographic metadata and full texts of Jarvis 2014, Claramunt & Cracraft 2015, Suh 2015, Kuhl 2021, Stiller 2024 and Wu 2024 were retrieved from PubMed/PMC in this session (DOIs below). Prum 2015 and Reddy 2017 were read only at abstract level (PubMed). Node ages marked "file-derived" were computed in this session by parsing the published dated-tree files deposited with Stiller et al. 2024 (method in the Inferences of question 3); they are not quoted from the papers' text.

## Q1. Data-type effects (coding vs noncoding, UCE, intron, 3'-UTR, intergenic) on Afroaves and Australaves basal relationships

### Takeaway
Coding exons are the data type most often at odds with the others at the base of Telluraves: they tend to pull the raptorial lineages (Accipitrimorphae, owls, falcons, seriemas) together into a "birds of prey" assemblage or place them sister to the other landbirds. Noncoding data (introns, UCEs, intergenic regions, 3'-UTRs) consistently recover Australaves (Cariamiformes + (Falconiformes + Psittacopasserae)). But they still disagree on the base of Afroaves: Accipitrimorphae alone vs. Accipitrimorphae + Strigiformes as sister to the rest, and where owls and mousebirds go. Stiller et al. 2024 make the specific point that exons violate model assumptions far more often than the other data types.

### Cited Findings
- **Jarvis et al. 2014 (48 genomes, TENT = introns + UCEs + exon codon positions 1–2; ~41.8 Mbp)**: the TENT recovered Australaves (seriemas, falcons, parrots, Passeriformes) with 100% BS and its sister Afroaves (Accipitrimorphae, owls, mousebirds, woodpeckers, bee-eaters…) — [Jarvis et al. 2014, Science, DOI 10.1126/science.1253451](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451) (PMC4405904, via PubMed).
- Jarvis 2014: "The monophyly of Afroaves was the only case of 100% BS in the ExaML TENT that conflicted with the MP-EST* TENT tree and involved a local shift in the owl with mousebirds and Accipitrimorphae birds of prey" — [Jarvis et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451).
- Jarvis 2014, coding data: the all-codon-position exon tree (c123, 13.3 Mbp) gave fully supported relationships incongruent with the intron (19.3 Mbp), TENT (37.4 Mbp) and whole-genome (322.1 Mbp) trees. It "associated species more with life history traits", including "a monophyletic clade of diurnal birds of prey and seriemas (albeit with low 40% BS)" — [Jarvis et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451).
- Jarvis 2014 mechanism: the third codon position showed the strongest violation of stationarity under GTR+GAMMA. RY-recoding c3 made the c123 tree more like the c12 tree. The 10% most compositionally heterogeneous exons (n = 830) tracked the c123/c3 trees, and their branch lengths correlated with GC content. The authors propose "convergent GC-biased gene conversion in exons" and conclude that genome-scale protein-coding data "were not only insufficient but were also misleading… due to convergence" — [Jarvis et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451).
- Jarvis 2014: the intron tree moved Phaethontimorphae to sister of core landbirds (86% BS), against 70% BS for sister to core waterbirds in the TENT. Noncoding introns "lend greater support for the TENT than the protein-coding and UCE sequences" — [Jarvis et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451).
- **Reddy et al. 2017**: the taxon-poor Jarvis matrix was 68% noncoding, while coding exons dominated the taxon-rich Prum matrix. A new matrix of 90% noncoding sequence for 235 species showed that "the most important variable was data type". Adding taxa increased congruence with the Jarvis (noncoding) tree, and the authors attribute the differences to "violations of the models used in phylogenetic analyses" — [Reddy et al. 2017, Syst Biol, DOI 10.1093/sysbio/syx041](https://doi.org/10.1093/sysbio/syx041) (abstract via PubMed).
- **Braun & Kimball 2021** (reanalysis of the Prum dataset, partitioned and RY-coded): "coding data tend to place at least some raptorial landbird lineages sister to the other core landbirds". The unpartitioned analysis of coding exons recovered the "raptors sister" topology, "a clade of all non-raptorial landbirds nested within a paraphyletic assemblage of raptors". All other nucleotide analyses of the full dataset and the coding exons "placed Accipitrimorphae (hawks, eagles, and vultures) sister to all other landbirds, placing owls and falcons elsewhere" — [Braun & Kimball 2021, Birds 2:1–22, DOI 10.3390/birds2010001](https://doi.org/10.3390/birds2010001) (PDF text read).
- Braun & Kimball 2021 list three surviving landbird hypotheses. (B) Australaves/Afroaves, "each of which has two raptorial lineages at their base", favoured by "the majority of recent analyses". (C) "splits raptors into four independent lineages, placing hawks, eagles, and New World vultures sister to the remaining core landbirds", found in the complete Prum dataset, in partitioned coding analyses, in the Kimball et al. MSC tree and in the original Prum et al. analyses. (D) "a 'core' raptorial clade at the base of landbirds and separate lineages for seriemas and falcons within Australaves", seen in their ASTRAL-RY tree and "strongly supported" in species-tree analyses of noncoding or combined data in other studies. Verdict: "it seems inappropriate to reject any of these three hypotheses at this time". The "paraphyletic assemblage at the base of core landbirds… can probably be rejected" — [Braun & Kimball 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/birds2010001).
- Braun & Kimball 2021: "RY coding also had an impact on raptor topology, with falcons and seriemas shifting to a position closer to the parrots and passerines". The raptorial landbirds "are all characterized by shorter branches than the remaining landbirds". The "topology of some of the deepest branches within core landbirds is one of the major uncertainties remaining in deep avian phylogeny", and the "position of mousebirds is also unclear" — [Braun & Kimball 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/birds2010001).
- **Kuhl et al. 2021 (3'-UTRs, 429 species, ~90% of families)**: the noncoding 3'-UTR and coding trees differed. In the CODON, CODON12 and amino-acid (AAS) trees, "the Falconiformes and Cariamiformes were moved away from the Passeriformes and Psittaciformes to form an assemblage of birds of prey". In the AAS tree "even the Strigiformes were enclosed in the birds of prey assemblage". The 3'-UTR tree recovered Australaves (Cariamiformes, Falconiformes, Psittaciformes, Passeriformes; Clade 7) and a "Higher Landbirds" clade (Clade 6) that "correspond[s] to the Afroaves" — [Kuhl et al. 2021, MBE, DOI 10.1093/molbev/msaa191](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msaa191) (PMC7783168).
- Kuhl 2021: "Only the branch leading to the Strigiformes in the Higher Landbirds had weaker support values for both SH-aLRT (88 for 100 gappiness, 32 for 110 gappiness) and UFBS (32)". Elsewhere: "the relationship of the Strigiformes (SH-aLRT: 88, respectively, 32; UFBS: 69) requires further attention". The UFBS value is given as both 32 and 69 in different places of the same paper. The authors suggest convergence in protein-coding sequences of birds of prey under similar selective pressures — [Kuhl et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msaa191).
- **Stiller et al. 2024 (363 genomes, 218 families; main tree from 63,430 intergenic loci, ASTRAL)**: the main tree supports Australaves vs Afroaves and "grouped Accipitriformes and Strigiformes as the sister to the remaining Afroaves, similar to previous coalescent-based analyses. Concatenated analyses, including ours, supported Accipitriformes alone as sister to the remaining Afroaves" — [Stiller et al. 2024, Nature 629:851–860, DOI 10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1) (PMC11111414).
- Stiller 2024 data-type comparison: a species tree from 14,355 exon loci (third positions excluded) "differed in 38 of 360 branches from the main tree (compared with six or seven differences for the other data types)". Loci at risk of saturation: exons 30.83%, intergenic 0.07%, UCEs 0.34%, introns 0.83%. Loci failing the stationarity test: exons 2.45%, UCEs 0.02%, intergenic 0.07%, introns 0.08%. Mean locus length (recombination risk): exons 16,964 bp, introns 2,543 bp, UCEs 2,095 bp, intergenic 897 bp — [Stiller et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1).
- Stiller 2024: remaining recalcitrant nodes "involve species that are a challenge to model due to either extreme DNA composition, variable substitution rates, incomplete lineage sorting or complex evolutionary events such as ancient hybridization" — [Stiller et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1).
- Stiller 2024 taxon-sampling effect: "the relationship between Accipitriformes and Strigiformes depended on the number of passeriform taxa sampled. The main topology was obtained only when at least 138 Passeriformes were included, whereas sampling fewer taxa of each order favoured Accipitriformes as the sister to the remaining Afroaves". In order-subsampling tests, Accipitriformes + Strigiformes was the strongest of only three cases in which taxon sampling changed ordinal relationships — [Stiller et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1).
- **Wu et al. 2024 (124 genomes; CDS, CNEE, intron, intergenic; NJst + RAxML)**: the "optimum tree" (CDS + introns + intergenic, after removing 30% outlier gene trees) places Coraciimorphae "as the most basal clade" of Telluraves and "uncovers a novel monophyletic clade, uniting Accipitriformes (hawks) and Strigiformes (owls), which we designate Hieraves", sister to Australaves. Under this tree Afroaves is not monophyletic. Concatenated trees of single DNA classes "exhibited substantial incongruences with one another", and CNEEs were excluded from the final combination — [Wu et al. 2024, PNAS 121:e2319696121, DOI 10.1073/pnas.2319696121](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2319696121) (PMC10895254).
- Wu 2024: the multispecies coalescent "accounts for 76.9%, 96.5%, 95.3%, and 87.7% of gene tree heterogeneity in CDS, CNEE, introns, and intergenic segments", i.e. the poorest MSC fit is in coding data — [Wu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2319696121).
- Topology check on the deposited dated trees (file-derived): in the Prum et al. 2015 chronogram, Accipitrimorphae (Cathartes, Vultur, Sagittarius, Pandion, Accipitridae) is sister to all other Telluraves, and Afroaves is not monophyletic (the MRCA of the Afroaves orders is the crown Telluraves node). In the Jarvis 2014 TENT chronogram, Accipitrimorphae + Tyto is not a clade: the barn owl is sister to the Coraciimorphae s.l. clade (mousebird, cuckoo-roller, trogon, hornbill, bee-eater, woodpecker). Source files: [Avian-TimeTree.tre (Prum tree)](https://sid.erda.dk/share_redirect/ENhZODU9YE/07_scripts_figures/EDF6a/Avian-TimeTree.tre), identified as the Prum tree in [EDF6a.R](https://sid.erda.dk/share_redirect/ENhZODU9YE/07_scripts_figures/EDF6a/EDF6a.R); [Chronogram01.TENT.ExAML.tre (Jarvis)](https://sid.erda.dk/share_redirect/ENhZODU9YE/07_scripts_figures/EDF6a/Chronogram01.TENT.ExAML.tre).

### Inferences
- Across studies, Australaves (Cariamiformes sister to Falconiformes + Psittacopasserae) is the most data-type-robust raptor-bearing node in noncoding and species-tree analyses. Coding-dominated concatenation is the main source of the "birds of prey" grouping (Jarvis c123, Kuhl CODON/AAS, Braun-Kimball unpartitioned exons). That grouping looks like convergence, driven by compositional heterogeneity and saturation in exons, rather than history.
- The unstable node is the base of Afroaves/Telluraves. Four placements of Accipitrimorphae are on record: alone sister to the rest of Afroaves (Jarvis TENT, Stiller concatenation); with owls sister to the rest of Afroaves (Stiller ASTRAL main tree, Jarvis MP-EST*); sister to all other Telluraves (Prum 2015, Braun-Kimball full Prum data); or with owls (Hieraves) sister to Australaves, with Coraciimorphae basal (Wu 2024). Owls switch between Accipitrimorphae and Coraciimorphae.
- Data type, the gene-tree vs concatenation method, and taxon sampling of an unrelated clade (Passeriformes) all change this node. This fits a short, near-unresolvable internode rather than one single bias.

### Gaps
- Reddy et al. 2017's specific results for Afroaves and Australaves were not read (paywalled full text; abstract only).
- Prum et al. 2015's text was not accessible (Nature paywall). Its landbird topology is inferred here from the deposited chronogram and from Braun & Kimball's description, not from Prum's own text or support values.
- Kuhl 2021's exact topology at the base of its "Higher Landbirds" clade (position of Accipitriformes vs Strigiformes vs Coraciimorphae) and the identity of the four data-sensitive nodes were not extracted, beyond the Strigiformes branch.
- No study found here reports GC3 or compositional-heterogeneity statistics specifically for falcons, seriemas or hawks. The "raptors have shorter branches" observation (Braun & Kimball 2021) is the only raptor-specific rate/branch statement.

## Q2. ILS, gene-tree/quartet discordance, hard polytomy, anomaly zone and concordance at the raptor nodes

### Takeaway
Retroposon data (Suh 2015) estimate 59–81% ILS on the three deepest post-K–Pg branches of core landbirds. They show owls nearly equally supported as sister to Accipitrimorphae or to Coraciimorphae, "approximat[ing] a trifurcation". In Stiller 2024 the hawk–owl node has two topologies with statistically indistinguishable quartet support (35% vs 34.6%) and a significantly lower third (30.5%), a pattern the authors read as possible ancient hybridization rather than ILS alone. The formal "hard polytomy" claim (Suh 2016) concerns the root of Neoaves, not the Telluraves raptor nodes. The anomaly zone has been raised but never tested.

### Cited Findings
- **Suh et al. 2015 (2,118 retroposon presence/absence markers)**: "the three deepest branches within the post-K-Pg diversification of core landbirds are affected by 59%–81% ILS, including the two branches involved in the aforementioned mousebird conflict". ILS-derived incongruences involve 35% of autosomal and 34% of Z-chromosome loci — [Suh et al. 2015, PLoS Biol 13:e1002224, DOI 10.1371/journal.pbio.1002224](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1002224) (PMC4540587).
- Suh 2015 on owls: the owl placement in the MPRE tree agrees with their earlier owl RE analysis. "However, we emphasize that an alternative grouping of owls with eagles and New World vultures received nearly as strong RE support… which suggests that the position of the owls may in fact approximate a trifurcation" — [Suh et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1002224).
- Suh 2015 on mousebirds: the strongest RE support (29 markers) is for mousebirds as sister to the remaining Afroaves. The six alternatives got two to eleven markers each, with four supporting the Jarvis topology (mousebirds sister to Coraciimorphae). The near-symmetric support suggests ILS, "not… hybridization", and that the Jarvis whole-genome/intron trees "recover a locally anomalous topology" — [Suh et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1002224).
- Suh 2015: Neoaves went through three radiations: a near-K–Pg "super-radiation" (73% of RE markers discordant), a core-landbird radiation in which "27% of the insertions… did not sort across mostly two to three speciation events", and a core-waterbird radiation with 18% total ILS. Super-radiations "may neither resemble a fully bifurcating tree nor are they resolvable as such" — [Suh et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1002224).
- **Jarvis 2014 transposable elements**: of 3,671 barn-owl LTR insertion loci, 61 were informative for owls among core landbirds. They showed "two dominant exclusive TE topologies: (i) an owl + Accipitrimorphae topology, as seen in the MP-EST* TENT; and (ii) an owl + Coraciimorphae topology that excludes mousebird, as seen in the UCE tree", plus nine minor topologies. By contrast, 13 TEs informative for Australaves were conflict-free (3 Passeriformes + parrots; 7 + falcons; 2 + seriemas). The authors read this as "higher ILS near the deepest branches of Afroaves involving the owl" — [Jarvis et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451).
- Jarvis 2014 indels: "the two branches joining mousebirds and owls exhibit[ed] the highest indel incongruence and the shortest internal branch lengths in the ExaML TENT". Branch length explained 87% of variation in indel incongruence (a hemiplasy/ILS signature) — [Jarvis et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451).
- Jarvis 2014 on the anomaly zone: "It is possible that some of the deep branches of the species tree are in the anomaly zone, although the gene tree support is not high enough to confidently test this idea" — [Jarvis et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451).
- **Stiller 2024 quartet frequencies at the Accipitriformes/Strigiformes node (node 26)**: "statistically indistinguishable for two topologies (35 versus 34.6%, test, P = 0.130), but the third was significantly lower (30.5%, P < 10^-x)… contradicting expectations of ILS". The exponent is lost in the PMC text extraction. With "no evidence of long branch attraction", the non-ILS pattern "could be indicative of ancestral hybridization". CoalHMM on alignment sites gave an ILS-like pattern (31.2% vs 29.6%). Because CoalHMM assumes ILS a priori, "an ancestral hybridization event, albeit too weak to be detected by CoalHMM, remains plausible" — [Stiller et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1).
- Stiller 2024: most of the ten clades (2.8%) that stayed incongruent even with 32,000 loci "were associated with short branches after the K–Pg boundary". Mousebirds "had an alternative placement in 30% of subsets of 32,000 loci" — [Stiller et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1).
- **Suh 2016 (Zoologica Scripta 45:50–62, DOI 10.1111/zsc.12213)** argues for a nine-taxon hard polytomy at the root of Neoaves from eight near-simultaneous speciation events coinciding with the K–Pg — [UEA research portal record](https://research-portal.uea.ac.uk/en/publications/the-phylogenomic-forest-of-bird-trees-contains-a-hard-polytomy-at/) (abstract-level, via search). This concerns the Neoaves root, where Telluraves is one of the lineages, not the raptor nodes inside Telluraves.
- Wu 2024: the low-confidence nodes in its tree were inside Aquaterraves. It reports that conflicting majority triplets among DNA classes were below 1%, and attributes low support to "a lack of sufficient phylogenetic signal". No Telluraves raptor node is flagged as low-support in the text — [Wu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2319696121).
- Internode lengths, file-derived from the Stiller 2024 main chronogram (median, 95% HPD): crown Telluraves 63.8 (63.0–65.0) Ma; crown Afroaves 63.6 (63.0–64.0); Accipitriformes + Strigiformes 63.4 (62.7–63.9); crown Australaves 63.1 (62.3–64.3); Falconiformes–Psittacopasserae 62.6 (61.7–63.8). That gives successive internodes of ~0.2–0.5 Myr, e.g. Afroaves → hawk–owl node ~0.2 Myr, Telluraves → Australaves ~0.7 Myr — [63k_dated.tre (Stiller Fig. 1 tree)](https://sid.erda.dk/share_redirect/ENhZODU9YE/07_scripts_figures/Fig1/63k_dated.tre).

### Inferences
- ILS and very short internodes are well documented as the main sources of conflict at the hawk/owl/mousebird nodes. At the hawk–owl node, Stiller's unequal minor-quartet frequencies leave room for ancient introgression. Model misspecification (exon composition and saturation) explains the coding-specific "birds of prey" artifacts. So the instability has more than one cause, and the causes differ between Afroaves (ILS ± hybridization, short branches) and the coding-driven raptor-grouping artifact (compositional convergence).
- The Australaves backbone carries much less ILS signal (conflict-free TE markers in Jarvis, consistent recovery across noncoding analyses). Afroaves' basal trichotomy (Accipitrimorphae / Strigiformes / Coraciimorphae) is the best candidate for a near-hard polytomy among the raptor nodes. No study has formally tested an anomaly zone or hard polytomy there.

### Gaps
- No gCF/sCF (IQ-TREE concordance factors) for these nodes were found in Jarvis, Prum, Kuhl, Stiller or Wu. Stiller reports ASTRAL quartet frequencies, Wu reports triplet frequencies. The per-node quartet values for the Australaves nodes (Stiller Fig. 2/EDF) were not extracted.
- A formal anomaly-zone test for Afroaves basal branches was not found.
- The Houde et al. 2019 indel study (cited in Braun & Kimball 2021) was not read in this session.

## Q3. Divergence times for raptor nodes across studies, and fossil calibrations used

### Takeaway
Two dating paradigms disagree by about 20 Myr. Jarvis 2014, Prum 2015, Claramunt & Cracraft 2015 and Stiller 2024 put crown Telluraves and all raptor-bearing splits within ~2–4 Myr after the K–Pg (crown Telluraves ≈ 63–64 Ma). Wu et al. 2024 put crown Telluraves at ~86 Ma in the Late Cretaceous. Crown ages of the raptor orders are much younger and less certain: crown Strigiformes ~46–56 Ma, crown Falconidae ~30–40 Ma. TimeTree summaries sit in between and are internally inconsistent for some pairs.

Other dated estimates (not in table):
- Claramunt & Cracraft 2015: 22 major neornithine groups, including the "predators (Cariamiformes and Falconiformes)", plus Coraciimorphae, Psittaciformes and Passeriformes, were reconstructed as present in South America "by the end of the middle Paleocene (~59.2 Ma)". The MRCA of modern birds is placed in South America ~95 Ma — [Claramunt & Cracraft 2015, Sci Adv 1:e1501005, DOI 10.1126/sciadv.1501005](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.1501005) (PMC4730849).
- Kuhl 2021 (DPPDiv, UGR model): Palaeognathae–Neognathae 94 Ma; Galloanserae–Neoaves 81 Ma. The divergence dates "conform with the fossil record of crown group… Accipitriformes", with the pandionine accipitriform fossils "from the early Oligocene, some 32–34 Ma". The authors note "none of the results is entirely congruent with the fossil record" — [Kuhl et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msaa191).
- Fuchs et al. 2015 (Falconidae, 8 loci, all 64 species, two falconid fossil calibrations): the three subfamilies Herpetotherinae, Polyborinae and Falconinae have "similar diversification ages in the Early to Middle Miocene". Falconinae began ~16 Ma and Falco ~7.5 Ma, with a diversification-rate increase at the base of Falco correlated with C4 grassland expansion — [Fuchs et al. 2015, MPE 82:166–182, DOI 10.1016/j.ympev.2014.08.010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ympev.2014.08.010) (PubMed abstract).
- Stiller 2024 text: only Mirandornithes (67.4 Ma; 66.2–68.9) and Columbaves (66.5; 65.2–67.9) diverged before the K–Pg. "All subsequent divergences postdate the boundary", although the 95% interval of the Telluraves–Elementaves divergence spans it — [Stiller et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1).

Calibration schemes:
- Jarvis 2014: first and second codon positions of 1,156 clock-like exons; "19 conservatively chosen avian fossils (plus nonavian outgroups) as minimum bounds", maximum bound 99.6 Ma for Neornithes; MCMCTREE autocorrelated relaxed clock on the fixed ExaML TENT — [Jarvis et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451).
- Prum 2015 (as described by Wu 2024): "the maximum bound of all avian fossil calibrations was set to 65 Ma, and the minimum and maximum constraints for the divergence of crown Aves were set to 60.5 and 86.5 Ma". Fifteen of Wu's 20 calibrations were taken from Prum — [Wu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2319696121).
- Claramunt & Cracraft 2015: empirically derived calibration priors from fossil-occurrence distributions; 10 calibration priors for the genome-scale (Jarvis 1,156-exon) dataset and 24 for the RAG1/RAG2 dataset (230 species); 130 fossil birds overall; BEAST 2 — [Claramunt & Cracraft 2015](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.1501005).
- Kuhl 2021: "Eighteen nodes in the tree were calibrated with fossil data, which were also used before" (the Jarvis set). The pigeon–Mirandornithes calibration was omitted because of topological differences — [Kuhl et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msaa191).
- Wu 2024: 20 fossil constraints, MCMCTree with CDS data. Crown Aves bounded by a minimum of 66.7 Ma (earliest crown bird) and a maximum of 94.3 Ma (earliest Ichthyornithes), with sensitivity runs using 105.3 and 122.8 Ma maxima. The authors attribute Jarvis's younger ages to sparse taxon sampling (node-density effect) and say Prum's ages "can only be obtained by imposing a strict restriction on the maximum bound… to 65 Ma" — [Wu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2319696121).
- Stiller 2024: "calibration densities for 34 nodes using 187 fossil occurrences… in a Bayesian sequential-subtree framework", with branch lengths from intergenic regions. "The widest credible intervals were observed for nodes positioned furthest from the calibration points, including the secondary calibrations involved in subtree dating" — [Stiller et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1).
- In the deposited MCMCtree files, the Afroaves subtree carries fossil calibrations named "Accipitriformes" (main: skew-t ST(59.868, 7.981, 17.620, 2.705); uniform-prior alternative B(58.94, 64.0311)), "Strigiformes" (ST(51.049, 6.467, 24.212, 2.615); B(50.54, 64.0311)), "Coraciimorphae" (B(62.24, …)), "Coracii", "Bucerotes" and "Todidae–Alcedinidae" — [subtree_fossil_calibrations.csv](https://sid.erda.dk/share_redirect/ENhZODU9YE/04_timetree/MCMCtree_run_files/subtree_fossil_calibrations.csv). The Afroaves subtree root prior is B(62.2, 63.8848) — [backbone_tree_fossil_calibrations.csv](https://sid.erda.dk/share_redirect/ENhZODU9YE/04_timetree/MCMCtree_run_files/backbone_tree_fossil_calibrations.csv). No calibration for Falconiformes, Cariamiformes or Psittaciformes appears among the subtree fossil calibrations; the only Australaves subtrees calibrated there are passerine.

### Inferences
- Method for file-derived ages: NEXUS trees were parsed in Python (custom parser). MRCAs of order/family taxon sets were taken using the Stiller species-to-order table ([annotations.txt](https://sid.erda.dk/share_redirect/ENhZODU9YE/07_scripts_figures/EDF6a/annotations.txt)) or genus lists. Node height is the root-to-tip depth of the ultrametric chronogram, and the 95% HPD is read from node annotations (Prum: BEAST height_median). Validation: crown Passeriformes matched published values in Stiller (50.7 Ma, exact) and Jarvis (~39 Ma).
- The very narrow Stiller intervals at the Telluraves/Afroaves/Australaves nodes (≈1–2 Myr) partly reflect the secondary subtree-root prior (Afroaves B(62.2, 63.88)). They should not be read as independent fossil precision; Stiller's own caveat on secondary calibrations points the same way.
- Wu 2024 conflicts with the others mainly in its maximum-bound and taxon-sampling choices: crown Telluraves ~86 Ma vs ~63–64 Ma. Raptor crown-order ages (owls, falcons, accipitrids) are much younger than their stems in every tree. The raptorial stem lineages therefore originated near the K–Pg (or earlier under Wu), while the crown radiations are Eocene–Miocene.
- TimeTree caveats: adjusted ages collapse several nested nodes to one value (70.6 Ma for Telluraves, Australaves, Falconiformes–Psittacopasserae and Psittacopasserae; 67.5 Ma for both owl stem and owl crown). Precomputed medians can invert the topology (Falconidae–Psittaciformes 74.2 Ma older than Cariamiformes–Passeriformes 67.3 Ma), and the two crown-Accipitridae pairs disagree (18.0 vs 42.8 Ma). TimeTree should be cited only as a literature summary, not as an estimate for these nodes.

### Gaps
- Node ages for raptor nodes from Claramunt & Cracraft 2015 (Fig. 2 MCC trees, table S2) and Kuhl 2021 (supplementary chronogram) were not extracted. Their text does not give these values.
- Wu 2024 gives only crown Telluraves among the raptor nodes in its text. Hieraves, Australaves and the order-level ages are in Fig. 2 / Datasets S01–S05, which were not read.
- The specific fossils used by Jarvis 2014 (SM12), Prum 2015 and Stiller 2024 for raptor nodes are not identified here. Stiller's "Accipitriformes" and "Strigiformes" calibrations (minima ~58.9 and ~50.5 Ma) are known only by name and density, and whether each applies to the crown or the stem was not confirmed.
- Crown Accipitridae (Elaninae vs the rest) is dated only in the Prum chronogram among the genome-scale studies. The Elaninae-first topology was not verified in this session.
- Prum 2015's published text values (e.g. its stated crown ages) were not seen; Prum numbers here come only from the deposited chronogram.

## Q4. Biogeography of Telluraves and raptors

### Takeaway
Two main frameworks exist. Ericson 2012 proposes three reciprocally monophyletic Gondwanan landbird radiations: Australian (passerines + parrots), South American (falcons + seriemas) and African (Afroaves, including owls, hawks and New World vultures); this is the origin of the names Australaves and Afroaves. Claramunt & Cracraft 2015 propose a West Gondwanan (South American) origin of Neoaves, with raptorial Cariamiformes and Falconiformes in South America by ~59 Ma. They reject an African origin of Coraciimorphae in favour of North American ancestors and North Atlantic dispersal.

### Cited Findings
- **Ericson 2012** (J Biogeogr 39:813–824): a dispersal–vicariance analysis of a family-level nuclear-DNA tree "showed that three reciprocally monophyletic groups of terrestrial birds have diversified in the Gondwanan land areas of Australia, South America and Africa". The "only survivors today from the Australian radiation are the passerines and parrots, while the falcons and seriemas have survived from the South American radiation". The African group "includes all coraciiform and piciform birds, owls, diurnal raptors (except falcons), New World vultures, trogons, mousebirds and cuckoo-rollers". The scenario is "consistent with… Late Cretaceous to early Palaeogene dates" and implies many convergent adaptations — [Ericson 2012, DOI 10.1111/j.1365-2699.2011.02650.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2699.2011.02650.x) (abstract via [Crossref API](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1111/j.1365-2699.2011.02650.x)).
- Ericson 2012 introduced the name Australaves (as "Australavis", p. 815) — [Wikispecies: Australaves](https://species.wikimedia.org/wiki/Australaves) (secondary).
- **Claramunt & Cracraft 2015**: the origin of Neornithes, Palaeognathae, Galloanseres and Neoaves is reconstructed as West Gondwana ("a united continental South America, West Antarctica, and portions of East Antarctica"). A diverse avifauna including "predators (Cariamiformes and Falconiformes)" was present in South America by ~59.2 Ma. Two main dispersal routes: a trans-Antarctic corridor to Australia/Zealandia, and expansion to the Old World through North America — [Claramunt & Cracraft 2015](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.1501005).
- Claramunt & Cracraft 2015: "instead of the Coraciimorphae arising in Africa… our optimizations indicate that Leptosomatiformes, Coliiformes, and Coraciiformes colonized the Paleotropics independently from North American ancestors", reaching the western Palearctic "through a North Atlantic corridor before ~52 Ma" — [Claramunt & Cracraft 2015](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.1501005).
- Jarvis 2014 recovered Australaves and Afroaves but cautioned: "our taxon sampling is insufficient to address the biogeographic justification of their names" — [Jarvis et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451).
- Fuchs et al. 2015: falcon (Falco) diversification is tied to Late Miocene expansion of open C4 habitats; grassland/savanna and migratory Falco lineages had higher diversification rates — [Fuchs et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ympev.2014.08.010).
- Wu 2024 links trans-K–Pg avian diversification with the Angiosperm Terrestrial Revolution. It reports that crown Telluraves (~86 Ma) diverged "nearly simultaneously" with Atlantogenata (~85.8 Ma), but it does not give a raptor-specific biogeographic reconstruction — [Wu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2319696121).

### Inferences
- Ericson's African-Afroaves hypothesis and Claramunt & Cracraft's West-Gondwana/North-America-to-Old-World model disagree for Afroaves: owls, hawks and Coraciimorphae arise in Africa in the first, while in the second at least Coraciimorphae came from Holarctic ancestors. They agree on a South American history for the falcon + seriema part of Australaves.
- The biogeographic inference for the Afroaves raptors depends on the unresolved basal Afroaves topology (Q1–Q2). If Accipitrimorphae or Hieraves are sister to Australaves (Wu 2024), the "Afroaves" region-based narrative loses its clade.

### Gaps
- No post-2015 formal ancestral-area reconstruction for the Telluraves backbone using the Stiller 2024 or Wu 2024 trees was found.
- Claramunt & Cracraft's node-by-node ancestral areas for Accipitriformes, Strigiformes and Cathartidae (their Fig. 3 and table S2) were not extracted.
- The biogeography of crown Accipitridae and Strigiformes (e.g. Old vs New World origins) was not covered by sources read here.

## Q5. Definition of "raptor/bird of prey": McClure et al. 2019 and responses

### Takeaway
McClure et al. 2019 define raptors phylogenetically: all species in orders that evolved from a raptorial landbird lineage and in which most species kept the raptorial lifestyle. In practice that is five orders: Accipitriformes, Strigiformes, Cathartiformes, Falconiformes and Cariamiformes. The basis is ancestry inferred from the Jarvis-style "apex-predator core-landbird ancestor", not shared traits or diet. Seriemas are the novel and debated addition. A 2022 seriema review by the same group calls their inclusion "nontraditional" and possibly "controversial".

### Cited Findings
- Citation: McClure C.J.W., Schulwitz S.E., Anderson D.L., Robinson B.W., Mojica E.K., Therrien J.F., Oleyar M.D., Johnson J. 2019. "Commentary: Defining raptors and birds of prey". Journal of Raptor Research 53(4):419–430, DOI 10.3356/0892-1016-53.4.419 — [BioOne record](https://bioone.org/journals/Journal-of-Raptor-Research/volume-53/issue-4/0892-1016-53.4.419/Commentary-Defining-Raptors-and-Birds-of-Prey/10.3356/0892-1016-53.4.419.full) (citation from search result; full text blocked to automated fetch).
- Definition, as quoted by Kenn Kaufman (Audubon, 11 Dec 2019): raptors are "all species within orders that evolved from a raptorial land bird lineage and in which most species maintained their raptorial lifestyle as derived from their common ancestor". In practice it "would apply only to these orders: Accipitriformes (hawks, eagles, etc.), Strigiformes (owls), Cathartiformes (New World vultures), Falconiformes (falcons and caracaras), and Cariamiformes (seriemas)" — [Kaufman 2019, Audubon (PDF copy)](https://files.constantcontact.com/4be1979f401/41677fb9-aefd-44ca-be06-93d2f29e09da.pdf); original at [audubon.org](https://www.audubon.org/news/ask-kenn-kaufman-falcons-hawks-owls-vultures-what-exactly-raptor).
- Basis: The Peregrine Fund's release (5 Dec 2019) says the definition rests on evolutionary ancestry, not physical or ecological traits. Talons and hooked beaks were judged ambiguous and able to evolve independently. Owls and vultures "most definitely qualify" (McClure), and seriemas "probably deserve membership" because they kill vertebrate prey and are the only surviving descendants of the "terror birds" — [Peregrine Fund news release](https://www.peregrinefund.org/news-release/improved-definition-what-makes-raptor-raptor-results-welcoming-new-species-flock).
- Rationale for excluding diet-based definitions: some raptors do not hunt vertebrates (e.g. Palm-nut Vulture), and some predatory birds such as skuas are not considered raptors — [search summary of McClure et al. 2019](https://bioone.org/journals/Journal-of-Raptor-Research/volume-53/issue-4/0892-1016-53.4.419/Commentary-Defining-Raptors-and-Birds-of-Prey/10.3356/0892-1016-53.4.419.pdf) (UNVERIFIED against the primary text). Kaufman discusses shrikes as hooked-billed predators that the definition does not include — [Kaufman 2019](https://files.constantcontact.com/4be1979f401/41677fb9-aefd-44ca-be06-93d2f29e09da.pdf).
- Phylogenetic premise: Jarvis 2014 inferred from "the non-monophyly of the birds of prey at the deepest branches of the Australaves and Afroaves radiations… that the common ancestor of core landbirds may have been an apex predator, followed by two losses of the raptorial trait". Seriemas "could be considered to belong to a raptorial taxon because they kill vertebrate prey… and are the sole living relatives of the extinct giant 'terror birds'". Mousebirds and cuckoo-roller have Eocene relatives "with raptor-like feet" — [Jarvis et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451).
- Follow-up: Méndez, Szymczycha, Sullivan & McClure 2022 (J Raptor Res 56(1):138), a literature assessment of seriemas, notes that viewing seriemas as raptors "is nontraditional and might be controversial" and that more information on evolution, taxonomy, morphology and ecology "will clarify the validity of their consideration as raptors" — [J Raptor Res 56(1) p.138](https://rapt.kglmeridian.com/view/journals/rapt/56/1/article-p138.xml) (UNVERIFIED: from search-result summary only; page returned HTTP 403).
- Taxonomic nuance: McClure et al. list Cathartiformes as a separate order. The genome-scale trees treat New World vultures (Cathartidae) as sister to the remaining Accipitriformes within Accipitrimorphae (e.g. the Stiller annotations classify Cathartes aura as Accipitriformes: Cathartidae) — [annotations.txt](https://sid.erda.dk/share_redirect/ENhZODU9YE/07_scripts_figures/EDF6a/annotations.txt); Jarvis: "eagles and New World vultures (now in Accipitrimorphae)" — [Jarvis et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451).

### Inferences
- The definition is phylogenetic in form (ancestry of orders), with an ecological filter ("most species maintained their raptorial lifestyle"). It therefore excludes Afroaves and Australaves orders that descend from the same inferred raptorial ancestor but lost the habit (Coraciimorphae, Psittaciformes, Passeriformes). That is a paraphyletic-by-design grouping, not a clade. Under every topology reviewed in Q1, the five orders never form one clade.
- The "raptorial ancestor" premise depends on raptorial lineages sitting at the base of both Australaves and Afroaves. The alternative topologies (Prum/Braun-Kimball "Accipitrimorphae sister to all other landbirds"; Wu 2024 "Coraciimorphae basal, Hieraves sister to Australaves") change how many gains or losses of the raptorial habit are needed. This was not re-analysed in any source found. The order list is robust to these alternatives; the ancestral-state narrative is not.

### Gaps
- The primary McClure et al. 2019 text could not be read: BioOne blocked the fetch and the PDF link returned a bot-protection page. Exact wording on excluded groups, species counts and cited phylogenies is therefore from secondary sources.
- No formal published critique or reply to McClure et al. 2019, beyond the 2022 seriema review, was found in one search.
- No formal ancestral-state reconstruction of the "raptorial" habit across Telluraves on the 2024 genome-scale trees was found.
