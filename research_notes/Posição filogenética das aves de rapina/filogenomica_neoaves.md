# Phylogenomic placement of the raptor orders (Cathartiformes/Accipitriformes, Falconiformes, Strigiformes) within Neoaves/Telluraves, 2006–2026

Notes for a meta-analysis. Every DOI/PMID below came from a tool result in this session (Europe PMC, PubMed, OpenAlex, Unpaywall, publisher/PMC pages). Values marked "read from figure" were transcribed by me from the published figure image (PMC/journal CDN), not from text; treat them as transcriptions that should be double-checked against the PDF before publication. "UNVERIFIED" = could not confirm from a primary source in this session.

Node shorthand used throughout:
- (a) Telluraves monophyletic and split into Afroaves + Australaves
- (b) Accipitrimorphae (Cathartidae + Sagittariidae + Pandionidae + Accipitridae) monophyletic
- (c) position of Accipitrimorphae (sister to rest of Afroaves / sister to all other Telluraves / with owls)
- (d) position of Strigiformes
- (e) Falconiformes sister to Psittacopasserae (= Eufalconimorphae)
- (f) Cariamiformes sister to Eufalconimorphae (= Australaves)
- (g) sister group of Telluraves
- "Hieraves" = Accipitriformes + Strigiformes (name from Wu et al. 2024)

## Key question 1 — Study-by-study evidence (data, taxa, methods, topology and support for nodes a–g)

### Takeaway
Telluraves, Accipitrimorphae, Eufalconimorphae/Psittacopasserae and (in non-coding/genomic data) Australaves are recovered by essentially every large molecular study since 2008, usually with maximal support; the contested part is the base of Afroaves — whether Accipitrimorphae is sister to the remaining Afroaves, sister to all other Telluraves, or sister to owls (Hieraves), and where Strigiformes sits — plus the sister group of Telluraves. The largest dataset to date (Stiller et al. 2024, 363 genomes) recovers Hieraves as sister to the remaining Afroaves in the coalescent main tree (LPP = 1) but Accipitriformes alone sister to the remaining Afroaves in concatenation, with near-equal quartet frequencies (35% vs 34.6% vs 30.5%), and 2025–2026 reanalyses of the same genomes do not recover Afroaves at all.

#### Per-study findings

**Ericson et al. 2006 (Biol Lett; PMID 17148284)**
- 87 neoavian species representing 75 families; 5,007 bp from five nuclear regions (c-myc, RAG-1, myoglobin, β-fibrinogen, ODC); main tree Bayesian, nodes with PP <95% collapsed — [Ericson et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsbl.2006.0523) (PMC page text via WebFetch).
- "Strongly supported is a previously unrecognized major clade" including "diurnal birds of prey, seriemas, parrots and the 'higher landbird assemblage'"; a clade of "the secretarybird and accipitrid diurnal birds of prey (osprey, hawks and allies) to the exclusion of falcons", recovered in four of five single-gene analyses; "The New World vultures clearly have their affinity with other raptors and not with storks" — [Ericson et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsbl.2006.0523). No numeric PP given in text (only the 0.95 collapse threshold).

**Hackett et al. 2008 (Science; PMID 18583609)**
- ~32 kb from 19 independent nuclear loci for 169 species; documented "a sister relationship between passerines and parrots" — [Hackett et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1157704) (abstract).
- Support values as compiled in Gatesy & Springer 2022 Fig. 2H (GARLI BS; read from figure): Telluraves 98%; Afroaves ≤70%; owl + mousebird ≤70%; (Cavitaves, (mousebird, owl)) ≤70%; Australaves 64%; falcon + parrots/passerines 73% — [Gatesy & Springer 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/genes13071167). Same panel gives Burleigh et al. 29-locus RAxML: Telluraves 49%, Afroaves 50%, owl+mousebird 42%, Australaves 72%, Eufalconimorphae 59%.
- Hackett's hawks + vultures node had "weak support" — [McCormack et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0054848); Eufalconimorphae and Psittacopasserae "received relatively moderate bootstrap support" in Hackett — [Suh et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms1448).

**Suh et al. 2011 (Nat Commun; PMID 21863010)** — see Key question 2 for marker counts — [Suh et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms1448).

**McCormack et al. 2013 (PLoS One; PMID 23382987)**
- UCEs from 32 Neoaves + chicken; 1,541-locus matrix (87% complete) and 416-locus complete matrix — [McCormack et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0054848).
- "Both ML and Bayesian trees from the 1,541 locus analysis provided perfect support for falcons sister to the parrot+passerine clade, whereas the representative of the hawk family was sister to the vultures with high support"; "The Bayesian tree placed owls sister to the 'near passerines' and then hawks+vultures sister to owls+'near passerines', a topology that also appeared in the ML tree with weak support"; mousebird placement unstable (sister to near-passerines with 416 loci, sister to passerines with 1,541 loci) — [McCormack et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0054848).
- Values in Gatesy & Springer 2022 Fig. 2I (PP/BS, read from figure): landbirds >0.95/>70%; owl+Cavitaves >0.95/41%; (Accipitrimorphae + owl+Cavitaves) 0.97/21%; mousebird + (falcon, parrots+passerines) >0.95/53%; falcon + parrots/passerines >0.95/>70% — [Gatesy & Springer 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/genes13071167).
- Landbirds and waterbirds "as sister clades in three of four trees" — [McCormack et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0054848).

**Jarvis et al. 2014 (Science; PMID 25504713, PMC4405904)**
- 48 species representing all neoavian orders; "TENT strongly confirmed the monophyly of two large closely related clades that we refer to as core landbirds (Telluraves) and core waterbirds (Aequornithia)"; "we found 100% BS for a previously more weakly supported clade (Australaves) containing seriemas…, falcons…, parrots…, and Passeriformes and a sister clade (Afroaves) containing Accipitrimorphae birds of prey, owls, mousebirds, woodpeckers, and bee eaters" — [Jarvis et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451).
- "The monophyly of Afroaves was the only case of 100% BS in the ExaML TENT that conflicted with the MP-EST* TENT tree and involved a local shift in the owl with mousebirds and Accipitrimorphae birds of prey" — [Jarvis et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451).
- Fig. 3A/B (read from figure): ExaML TENT — Strigiformes + Coraciimorphae (node G) 84% BS (88% with third codon positions per legend convention); Telluraves + (Aequornithia + Phaethontimorphae) node R 96 (93); all other Telluraves nodes 100. MP-EST* TENT — owl + Accipitrimorphae (node a) unlabelled = 100%; (Coraciimorphae + Australaves) (node b) 91% — [Jarvis et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451). Gatesy & Springer's compilation lists 90% for node b — [Gatesy & Springer 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/genes13071167) (minor discrepancy; check PDF).
- Other Jarvis trees as compiled in Gatesy & Springer 2022 Fig. 2 (read from figure): intron RAxML — Afroaves 100, owl+Coraciimorphae 59, Coliiformes+Cavitaves 100; UCE RAxML — mousebird sister to rest of Afroaves (100), (Accipitrimorphae,(owl,Cavitaves)) 78, owl+Cavitaves 78; TENT MP-EST unbinned — owl+Accipitrimorphae 99, that pair + seriema 67, (Eufalconimorphae + Coraciimorphae) 79; protein-coding ExaML / amino-acid ExaML — seriema+owl 48%/94%, + Accipitrimorphae 50%/94%; intron ASTRAL PP / intron MP-EST BS — owl+Accipitrimorphae 1.00/70%, (Coraciimorphae + Australaves) 0.89/70%; UCE ASTRAL — owl+Accipitrimorphae 0.96, + Cavitaves 0.96, mousebird + Australaves 0.85 — [Gatesy & Springer 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/genes13071167).
- Phaethontimorphae "moved from being sister to core waterbirds with 70% BS in the TENT … to sister to core landbirds with 86% BS in the intron tree" — [Jarvis et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451).
- "The non-monophyly of the birds of prey at the deepest branches of the Australaves and Afroaves radiations suggests that the common ancestor of core landbirds may have been an apex predator, followed by two losses of the raptorial trait" — [Jarvis et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451).

**Kuhl et al. 2021 (MBE; PMID 32781465, PMC7783168)**
- 3′-UTRs of 221 family-level taxa, 379 genera, 429 species; SH-aLRT = 100 for most branches (98.2% at gappiness 110), UFBS = 100 for 93.6% — [Kuhl et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msaa191).
- "Only the branch leading to the Strigiformes in the Higher Landbirds had weaker support values"; Fig. 2 (read from figure) labels that node 89 (SH-aLRT, gappiness 110), 32 (gappiness 100), 69 (UFBS). The text gives inconsistent versions ("88 for 100 gappiness, 32 for 110 gappiness … UFBS (32)" in one paragraph; "SH-aLRT: 88, respectively, 32; UFBS: 69" in another) — [Kuhl et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msaa191).
- Fig. 2 topology (read from figure): Telluraves = Australaves (Cariamiformes, (Falconiformes, (Psittaciformes, Passeriformes))) + Higher Landbirds (Accipitriformes, (Strigiformes, (Coliiformes, Cavitaves))); Telluraves sister to "Aquatic & Semiaquatic Birds" (Aequornithes + Eurypygiformes + Phaethontiformes) — [Kuhl et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msaa191). Stiller et al. 2024 independently describe refs 1 (Jarvis) and 3 (Kuhl) as placing Phaethoquornithes sister to landbirds, and as concatenated analyses supporting Accipitriformes alone sister to remaining Afroaves — [Stiller et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1).
- Fig. 3A (read from figure): Accipitriformes = (Cathartidae [Cathartes melambrotus], (Sagittariidae [Sagittarius serpentarius], (Pandionidae [Pandion haliaetus], Accipitridae [Accipiter nisus]))) — [Kuhl et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msaa191).
- "In the CODON, CODON12, and the AAS trees, the Falconiformes and Cariamiformes were moved away from the Passeriformes and Psittaciformes to form an assemblage of birds of prey… In the AAS tree, even the Strigiformes were enclosed in the birds of prey assemblage" — [Kuhl et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msaa191). Coalescent analysis of 3′-UTR gene trees (up to 5,127) "disarranged" several clades, attributed to short single-gene alignments — [Kuhl et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msaa191).

**Braun & Kimball 2021 (Birds)**
- "We recovered the 'raptors sister' topology (i.e., a clade of all non-raptorial landbirds nested within a paraphyletic assemblage of raptors) in the unpartitioned analysis of coding exons… In contrast, all other analyses of nucleotide data for the complete dataset and the coding exons placed Accipitrimorphae (hawks, eagles, and vultures) sister to all other landbirds, placing owls and falcons elsewhere" — [Braun & Kimball 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/birds2010001).
- "The ASTRAL tree based on gene trees estimated using nucleotide sequences … placed core landbirds sister to all other Neoaves… in contrast to all other analyses" — [Braun & Kimball 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/birds2010001).
- Three competing Telluraves topologies: (B) Australaves + Afroaves, "the majority of recent analyses [6,8,11,13,16]" (Hackett, Kimball 2013, Jarvis, Reddy, Kuhl); (C) Accipitrimorphae sister to other core landbirds (complete Prum dataset, partitioned coding data, Kimball et al. 2013 MSC tree, original Prum analyses); (D) "a 'core' raptorial clade at the base of landbirds and separate lineages for seriemas and falcons within Australaves" (MSC trees incl. Jarvis species trees and Houde et al.); "the topology of some of the deepest branches within core landbirds is one of the major uncertainties remaining in deep avian phylogeny" — [Braun & Kimball 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/birds2010001).

**Gatesy & Springer 2022 (Genes; PMID 35885951)** — see Key question 2.

**Stiller et al. 2024 (Nature; PMID 38560995, PMC11111414)**
- 363 species, 218 families (92%); main data 63,430 purely intergenic 1-kb loci (63.43 Mb); ASTRAL main tree; concatenated tree "differed in only ten of the 360 branches"; main tree had three nodes <1.00 PP, concatenation seven nodes <100% BS — [Stiller et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1).
- Fig. 1a (read from figure; legend: LPP shown only if <1): only 0.90 (Strisores), 0.87 (Opisthocomiformes) and 0.95 (palaeognaths) are labelled, so Telluraves, Afroaves, Australaves and Accipitriformes + Strigiformes have LPP = 1 — [Stiller et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1).
- "Within Telluraves our main tree supported the proposed split into Australaves and Afroaves1,3 in contrast to other studies2,11 [Prum; Wu]. Our tree grouped Accipitriformes and Strigiformes as the sister to the remaining Afroaves… Concatenated analyses1,3, including ours, supported Accipitriformes alone as sister to the remaining Afroaves… quartet frequencies that were statistically indistinguishable for two topologies (35 versus 34.6%, χ2 test, PBenjamini–Hochberg adjusted = 0.130), but the third was significantly lower (30.5%, P < 10−16; node 26…), contradicting expectations of ILS" — [Stiller et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1).
- "The main topology was obtained only when at least 138 Passeriformes were included, whereas sampling fewer taxa of each order favoured Accipitriformes as the sister to the remaining Afroaves"; "Strigiformes and Accipitriformes were only recovered as a clade when more than 10 taxa were sampled" — [Stiller et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1).
- Telluraves sister = Elementaves (new clade: Aequornithes, Phaethontimorphae, Strisores, Opisthocomiformes, Cursorimorphae), "supported in coalescent-based analyses of intergenic regions and UCEs, but not by exons, introns or in concatenated analysis"; concatenation grouped Strisores sister to Telluraves "with low support (bootstrap = 32)"; "Support for Telluraves + Elementaves decreased with increasing taxon sampling" — [Stiller et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1).
- Adding exon-overlapping windows (94,402 loci) kept the four major clades (Mirandornithes, Columbaves, Telluraves, Elementaves) but changed five difficult branches — [Stiller et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1).

**Wu et al. 2024 (PNAS; PMID 38346181, PMC10895254)**
- Genomes of 124 species; 25,460 loci: 5,756 CDS, 12,449 CNEEs, 4,871 introns, 2,384 intergenic segments; NJst species trees; CNEEs excluded; "optimum tree" from CDS + introns + intergenic after removing 30% outlier gene trees — [Wu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2319696121).
- "Within Telluraves, the optimum tree supports … Coraciimorphae … as the most basal clade… a novel monophyletic clade, uniting Accipitriformes (hawks) and Strigiformes (owls), which we designate Hieraves. The Hieraves are sister to Australaves" — [Wu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2319696121).
- Telluraves vs Aquaterraves as the basal neoavian split; Aquaterraves BS = 78% in full data, 100% after removing ≥25% outlier gene trees; Fig. 2 shows only BS ≤90, and no value is printed in Telluraves (read from figure), i.e. Hieraves and Hieraves + Australaves >90% — [Wu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2319696121). In Fig. 2, turkey vulture groups with the accipitrids inside Hieraves (read from figure).
- Critique: ASTRAL-III reanalysis of Wu's cleaned 9,108 gene trees "conflicted at >10% of nodes, and Aquaterraves were not supported"; 768 CDS gene-tree branches of 34.54 substitutions/site traced to misalignment/orthology errors; intergenic segments contained CR1 paralogs — [Springer & Gatesy 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2406494121). Reply: after excluding 991 CDS and 180 intergenic long-branch gene trees, NJst tree unchanged; ASTRAL tree put Columbiformes in Aquaterraves, "diverging from Springer and Gatesy's ASTRAL tree, which placed Columbiformes within Telluraves" — [Wu et al. 2024 reply](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2409344121).

**Gupta, Mirarab & Turakhia 2025 (ROADIES; PNAS; PMID 40314967)**
- Automated species tree from 363 raw avian assemblies, 62,413 gene trees; agrees with Stiller on relationships within Australaves; "ROADIES places Accipitrimorphae as sister to all other land birds (Telluraves)"; contentious ordinal branches "had quartet frequencies that were close to ⅓" — [Gupta et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2500553122).

**Şapcı, Arasti, Braun & Mirarab 2026 (Phlag; Bioinformatics; PMID 42412820)**
- On Stiller's gene trees, branch 27 (Afroaves) "is so unstable that a wASTRAL reanalysis of the Stiller et al. (2024) gene trees fails to recover it. Instead, it puts Accipitriformes sister to all landbirds"; outlier-region gene trees moved Accipitriformes "to a clade including Strigiformes and several other orders"; "consistent with one or more reticulation events at the base of landbirds" — [Şapcı et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btag273).

**Chen et al. 2025 (morphology, Integr Org Biol; PMID 40917309)** — non-molecular, included for context
- 203 pectoral-girdle/forelimb characters, 75 species; when deep relationships were constrained to a phylogenomic consensus or to Braun & Kimball 2021, analyses 8, 9, 11 and 12 recovered "Accipitrimorphae … as the sister group to Strigiformes, forming Hieraves", in some analyses excluded from a clade with all other telluravians — [Chen et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/iob/obaf029).

**Accipitrimorphae / Accipitriformes composition (node b)**
- Cathartidae + Accipitridae: hawk sister to vultures "with high support", "improving upon the weak support for hawks+vultures from Hackett" — [McCormack et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0054848). Jarvis TENT: Turkey vulture + eagles unlabelled = 100% (Fig. 3A, read from figure) — [Jarvis et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451). 192 conflict-free retrotransposons (Suh 2015, Key question 2).
- Family order: Cathartidae sister to (Sagittariidae, (Pandionidae, Accipitridae)) in the 3′-UTR tree (Fig. 3A, read from figure) — [Kuhl et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msaa191); Cathartidae "recovered as the sister group to all other Accipitriformes" and chromosome painting shows "a similar genomic bauplan amongst Accipitridae and Pandionidae (ospreys), but one that differed from the basal-most Cathartidae" — [Canesin et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-70305-w).

### Inferences
- Nodes (a) Telluraves, (b) Accipitrimorphae, (e) Eufalconimorphae and Psittacopasserae are robust: supported by every sequence dataset (except unpartitioned coding/amino-acid trees for some), by large numbers of conflict-free retroelements, and by the 363-genome B10K tree.
- Node (f) Australaves is robust in non-coding, genome-scale and retroelement data (176 conflict-free REs) but is broken up in coding/protein trees (Kuhl CODON/AAS; Jarvis protein-coding ExaML; Braun & Kimball unpartitioned exons) and in some MSC/indel trees (Jarvis MP-EST unbinned; Houde UCE indels), i.e. it is a data-type-sensitive node, not an ILS-dominated one.
- Nodes (c) and (d) — the "Afroaves base" — are the genuinely unresolved part. Four topologies each have at least one high-support analysis: Accipitrimorphae sister to rest of Afroaves (Jarvis TENT, Kuhl, Kimball supertree; concatenation); Accipitrimorphae (alone or with owls) sister to all other Telluraves (Prum, Braun & Kimball, Houde indels, ROADIES, wASTRAL on B10K data); Hieraves sister to rest of Afroaves (Stiller main); Hieraves sister to Australaves (Wu). Owls have been placed as sister to Accipitrimorphae, Coraciimorphae, Cavitaves, Coliiformes and Cariamiformes.
- Concatenation vs coalescent is a consistent axis of conflict: concatenated genome-scale trees (Jarvis ExaML, Kuhl, Stiller RAxML-NG) put Accipitriformes alone sister to remaining Afroaves with owls next to Coraciimorphae, whereas coalescent summaries of the same or similar data (Jarvis MP-EST*/ASTRAL, Stiller ASTRAL, Houde) tend to unite owls with Accipitriformes.
- Node (g) is unresolved: Phaethoquornithes (Jarvis, Kuhl), hoatzin (Prum), Elementaves (Stiller), Aquaterraves (Wu), "waterbirds" (McCormack) or all other Neoaves (Braun & Kimball ASTRAL-NT); Houde et al. treat Telluraves as one of six lineages of a basal hard polytomy.

### Gaps
- Prum 2015, Reddy 2017 and Hackett 2008 full texts were not accessible (paywalled, no PMC copy); their node-level values here come from Gatesy & Springer 2022 Fig. 2 and Braun & Kimball 2021. Prum's exact BS (reported only as "≥70%") and Hackett's values for Cathartidae + Accipitridae and for the Telluraves sister group are UNVERIFIED.
- Ericson 2006: position of owls and numeric posterior probabilities not available from the text (figure only) — UNVERIFIED.
- Stiller 2024: the LPP for Accipitriformes + Strigiformes is inferred from the Fig. 1a convention (values <1 printed), not stated in text; the concatenated BS for "Accipitriformes alone sister to remaining Afroaves" (Extended Data Fig. 4) was not readable at available resolution — UNVERIFIED.
- Wu 2024: exact BS for Hieraves not printed (only ">90" by the figure convention).
- Mirarab et al. 2024 (PNAS; chromosome-4 low-recombination region) contained no statements about raptor nodes when searched — [Mirarab et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2319506121). A 2026 bioRxiv preprint on whole-genome-alignment guide-tree bias (Tao & Grünewald, DOI 10.64898/2026.07.06.736671) and Catanach et al. 2024 (UCEs within Accipitridae, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/biolinnean/blae028)) were found but not read.
- No 2024–2026 study with new data and a dedicated focus on the inter-ordinal raptor nodes was found beyond Stiller 2024, Wu 2024, and the reanalyses (ROADIES 2025, Phlag 2026).

## Key question 2 — Rare genomic changes (retroposons, indels, chromosomal rearrangements): which nodes, how many markers

### Takeaway
Retroelement presence/absence gives strong, nearly conflict-free support to Telluraves, Australaves, Eufalconimorphae, Psittacopasserae and Accipitrimorphae (tens to ~200 markers each in Suh et al. 2015), but at the base of Afroaves it splits between owl + Coraciimorphae/Cavitaves and owl + Accipitrimorphae (22 vs 15 TEs in Jarvis 2014) and shows asymmetries interpreted as possible ancient introgression involving owls (Gatesy & Springer 2022). Indels (Houde et al. 2019) favour owl + Accipitrimorphae as sister to all other Telluraves.

### Cited Findings
- Suh et al. 2011: "examined ~200,000 retroposon-containing loci… and retrieved 51 markers"; landbird assemblage 2 REs (branch G); "all 'landbirds' to the exclusion of mousebirds" 2 REs (branch H); seriemas + falcons + parrots + passerines 2 REs (branch I) — statistical testing "not applicable" for these three because of some incongruent patterns; Eufalconimorphae "seven REs, P=0.0005, (7 0 0), likelihood ratio test"; Psittacopasserae "three REs, P=0.0370, (3 0 0)" — [Suh et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms1448). All evidence for Eufalconimorphae and Psittacopasserae came from LTR elements — [Suh et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms1448).
- Jarvis et al. 2014: "Of 3671 barn owl long terminal repeat TE insertion loci orthologous in all of the bird genomes, 61 were informative for owls among core landbirds and showed two dominant exclusive TE topologies: (i) an owl + Accipitrimorphae topology… and (ii) an owl + Coraciimorphae topology that excludes mousebird"; Fig. 3G (read from figure) shows 22 TEs for owl + Coraciimorphae (excl. mousebird) and 15 for owl + Accipitrimorphae, with nine other topologies supported by fewer. For Australaves, of 25 informative TEs from the Suh 2011 screen, 13 were informative: "3 were exclusive for Passeriformes + parrots, 7 for Passeriformes + parrots + falcons, and 2 for the latter group plus seriemas, with no alternative topologies" — [Jarvis et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451).
- Jarvis et al. 2014 indels: 5.7 million indels scored; "the two branches joining mousebirds and owls exhibiting the highest indel incongruence and the shortest internal branch lengths" — [Jarvis et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451).
- Suh et al. 2015: 2,118 retrotransposon presence/absence markers on 48 genomes; MPRE tree "fully congruent with a genome-level tree based on sequences from UCEs" within core landbirds; "the placement of owls in the MPRE tree … is in agreement with our preliminary analysis of owl REs… However … an alternative grouping of owls with eagles and New World vultures received nearly as strong RE support…, which suggests that the position of the owls may in fact approximate a trifurcation"; "The strongest support (29 RE markers) was found for mousebirds as the sister taxon of the remaining Afroaves", six alternatives 2–11 markers each, 4 supporting the Jarvis mousebird + Coraciimorphae topology; "the three deepest branches within the post-K-Pg diversification of core landbirds are affected by 59%–81% ILS"; core-landbird radiation 27% total ILS — [Suh et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1002224).
- Suh et al. 2015 Fig. 1B (MPRE; ILS-free conflict-free insertions, ILS-affected counts in parentheses; read from figure): Telluraves 64 (91); Australaves 176 (1); Eufalconimorphae 88 (6); Psittacopasserae 16; Accipitrimorphae 192; Afroaves 16 (37); Afroaves minus mousebird 29 (79); owl + Cavitaves 25 (4); Cavitaves 124 (22) — [Suh et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1002224).
- Gatesy & Springer 2022 (quartet-based coalescent reanalysis of the RI matrix, 48 taxa, 2,113 characters): both RI trees "place Strigiformes as sister to Cavitaves… with Accipitriformes… and Coliiformes… branching from successively deeper nodes within a monophyletic Afroaves"; for Telluraves "nine clades had high Bayesian local PPs (0.95–1.0) and bootstrap support scores (92—100%). One exception is the Strigiformes + Cavitaves clade… (0.78 PP, 85% bootstrap)"; RI indices showed "an excess of support for Strigiformes + Accipitriformes relative to Cavitaves + Accipitriformes"; KKSC network with owl as hybrid of cuckoo-roller and eagle lineages p = 0.046 (and p = 0.014 on the 4-LIN subset) — [Gatesy & Springer 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/genes13071167).
- Houde et al. 2019: ">5 million indels from >7000 pan-genomic intronic and ultraconserved element (UCE) loci in 48 representatives"; "The position of owl+Accipitrimorphae as sister to all other Telluraves in the indel tree is better supported than it is as sister to Coraciimorphae in the nt tree (100% vs. 78%)"; adding indels "moved owl+Accipitrimorphae from Afroaves to sister to all other Telluraves, improving support for its new position from 78% to 100%"; combined intron nt + indel raised Australaves + Coraciimorphae from 89% to 97%; in the UCE indel tree the seriema's "inclusion with owl+Accipitrimorphae causes Psittacopasseria to be rejected with high support" — [Houde et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/d11070108).
- Chromosomal rearrangements: synteny of harpy eagle, goshawk, golden eagle vs California condor found "23 chromosome fissions… 19 tandem fusions… and 3 intrachromosomal translocations"; "most chromosomal rearrangements detected (n = 45) were found exclusively within the Accipitridae family"; Accipitridae + Pandionidae share a rearranged bauplan different from Cathartidae — [Canesin et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-70305-w).

### Inferences
- The best-supported raptor-relevant nodes by rare genomic changes are Accipitrimorphae (~192 conflict-free REs) and Australaves (~176) — both larger than the RE support for Telluraves itself (64, with 91 ILS-affected).
- Rare-genomic-change data do not settle (c)/(d): REs favour owl + Cavitaves (Suh 2015; Gatesy & Springer 2022) while indels favour owl + Accipitrimorphae (Houde 2019), and RE asymmetries suggest the owl lineage may carry an introgression signal, consistent with Stiller's non-ILS quartet pattern at the same node.
- Chromosome architecture supports Cathartidae as the earliest-diverging accipitriform lineage (shared ancestral-like synteny with Galloanserae), consistent with sequence trees.

### Gaps
- Per-node RE counts from Suh 2015 Fig. 1 and Jarvis Fig. 3G are figure transcriptions; S1 Table of Suh 2015 not examined.
- No retroelement study focused on Sagittariidae/Pandionidae placement was found.

## Key question 3 — What changed between studies and what authors say causes the conflict

### Takeaway
Authors attribute the conflicts mainly to (1) data type — coding exons and amino acids pull raptorial lineages together or to the base of Telluraves, while non-coding data recover Australaves/Afroaves; (2) very short internodes with high ILS at the base of Telluraves/Afroaves; (3) possible ancient hybridization involving owls/Accipitriformes; (4) analysis method (concatenation vs coalescent; gene-tree error); and (5) taxon sampling, which in B10K data flips the Accipitriformes–Strigiformes relationship.

### Cited Findings
- Data type: "the most important variable was data type"; incongruence reflects "data-type effects due to violations of the models used in phylogenetic analyses" — [Reddy et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1093/sysbio/syx041). "Coding data tend to place at least some raptorial landbird lineages sister to the other core landbirds"; coding-exon indicator clades include "a paraphyletic assemblage of raptors sister to the other landbirds"; RY coding shifted falcons and seriemas closer to parrots + passerines — [Braun & Kimball 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/birds2010001). Exons "had less phylogenetic information and were more variable in their signal", adding exon-overlapping windows changed five difficult branches, and "the damaging effects of model violation and limited signal of exons are not offset by increased taxon sampling" — [Stiller et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1). CODON/CODON12/AAS trees formed a birds-of-prey assemblage of Falconiformes + Cariamiformes (+ Strigiformes in AAS) — [Kuhl et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msaa191).
- Branch-length/long-branch artefacts: raptorial taxa (Accipitriformes, Cariamiformes, Falconiformes, Strigiformes) have short branches within Telluraves, and clustering of long-branched Psittacopasserae and Picocoraciae may reflect long-branch attraction — [Yuri et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.3390/biology2010419); "raptorial landbirds which are all characterized by shorter branches than the remaining landbirds" — [Braun & Kimball 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/birds2010001).
- ILS: "higher ILS near the deepest branches of Afroaves involving the owl" — [Jarvis et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451); three deepest core-landbird branches 59–81% ILS — [Suh et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1002224).
- Hybridization: quartet frequencies 35% vs 34.6% vs 30.5% at the Accipitriformes/Strigiformes node "contradicting expectations of ILS… the non-ILS patterns could be indicative of ancestral hybridization", though CoalHMM gave an ILS-like 31.2% vs 29.6% — [Stiller et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1); KKSC/4-LIN networks with owl as hybrid taxon — [Gatesy & Springer 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/genes13071167); spatially clustered discordant genomic blocks at the Afroaves branch "consistent with one or more reticulation events at the base of landbirds" — [Şapcı et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btag273).
- Method: ExaML TENT vs MP-EST* TENT differ by a "local shift in the owl with mousebirds and Accipitrimorphae" — [Jarvis et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451); "Concatenated analyses1,3, including ours, supported Accipitriformes alone as sister to the remaining Afroaves" vs coalescent Hieraves — [Stiller et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1); NJst vs ASTRAL and alignment/orthology errors (Wu vs Springer & Gatesy) — [Springer & Gatesy 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2406494121), [Wu et al. 2024 reply](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2409344121); coalescent analysis of short 3′-UTR gene trees "disarranged" clades — [Kuhl et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msaa191).
- Taxon sampling: Hieraves recovered "only when at least 138 Passeriformes were included"; "this case demonstrates that the effect of taxon sampling of one group can extend to others and that these sampling effects are not easily predictable" — [Stiller et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1). Increasing taxa increased congruence with the Jarvis (non-coding-rich) tree — [Reddy et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1093/sysbio/syx041).
- Telluraves sister group changed with each major study: Phaethoquornithes (Jarvis TENT 96 (93); Kuhl) → hoatzin (Prum) → Elementaves (Stiller; supported only by coalescent intergenic/UCE analyses) → Aquaterraves (Wu; BS 78 → 100 after outlier removal, not supported by ASTRAL reanalysis) — [Jarvis et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451); [Prum et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature15697); [Stiller et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1); [Wu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2319696121); [Springer & Gatesy 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2406494121). Houde et al. reduce the basal neoavian polytomy to "six major lineages, i.e., core landbirds (Telluraves), pan-waterbirds…, Charadriiformes, Gruiformes, Hoatzin, and Caprimulgiformes" — [Houde et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/d11070108). Gatesy & Springer: ten high-conflict basal clades with local PP 0.13–0.80 and BS 3–32% — [Gatesy & Springer 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/genes13071167).
- Mousebird (Coliiformes) instability co-varies with the owl/Accipitrimorphae problem: alternative placement in 30% of 32,000-locus subsets in B10K data — [Stiller et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1); placed sister to passerines with 1,541 UCEs — [McCormack et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0054848); unstable in 30 nuclear loci — [Wang et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msr230).

### Inferences
- For a meta-analysis, coding vs non-coding should be a moderator: almost every topology that breaks Australaves or unites falcons/seriemas with other raptors comes from coding/protein matrices, while non-coding/genome-scale matrices converge on Australaves + Afroaves.
- At the base of Afroaves the signal is close to a three-way tie (quartet frequencies ~1/3 in ROADIES; 35/34.6/30.5 in Stiller), so support values from single analyses (100% BS, LPP 1) overstate certainty; reticulation is now the leading explanation in 2024–2026 papers.

### Gaps
- No study quantifies how much of the Afroaves-base conflict is due to the chromosome-4 low-recombination region identified by Mirarab et al. 2024; that paper's analysis did not mention raptor nodes — [Mirarab et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2319506121).

## Key question 4 — Clade names and who defined them

### Takeaway
Psittacopasserae and Eufalconimorphae were named by Suh et al. 2011; Australaves derives from Ericson's (2011 online/2012) "Australavis", emended by Yuri et al. 2013; Telluraves and Cavitaves were proposed by Yuri et al. 2013; Afroaves is attributed to Ericson 2012; Hieraves and Aquaterraves are from Wu et al. 2024 and Elementaves from Stiller et al. 2024. The original authorship of Accipitrimorphae and Coraciimorphae was not confirmed. Sangster et al. 2022 gave PhyloCode definitions for 25 higher-level bird clade names.

### Cited Findings
- Eufalconimorphae ("true Falconimorphae"; falcons + parrots + passerines) and Psittacopasserae (parrots + passerines) are introduced as "new taxa" — [Suh et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms1448).
- Ericson's biogeographic paper divides terrestrial birds into an Australian radiation (passerines, parrots), a South American radiation (falcons, seriemas), and an African radiation including "all coraciiform and piciform birds, owls, diurnal raptors (except falcons), New World vultures, trogons, mousebirds and cuckoo‐rollers" — [Ericson 2012 (J Biogeogr, online 2011)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2699.2011.02650.x). Braun & Kimball cite this paper [68] for Afroaves — [Braun & Kimball 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/birds2010001).
- Yuri et al. 2013 Table 1: Australaves [cited to Ericson] ("The name 'Australavis', the spelling published by Ericson [66], was modified to have the more appropriate ending '-es'"); Telluraves ("Landbirds" of Hackett 2008), Cavitaves, Picodynastornithes, Litoritelluraves and Austrodyptornithes listed without priority citation; "We have also proposed names for as yet unnamed clades"; clades defined as "the least inclusive clade comprising the relevant species in the Early Bird tree" — [Yuri et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.3390/biology2010419). Eucavitaves listed as "(CPBT clade [30])" and Picocoraciae cited to Mayr — [Yuri et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.3390/biology2010419).
- Picocoraciae (non-leptosomid "Coraciiformes" + Piciformes) and Aequornithes introduced by Mayr — [Mayr 2011 (JZSER, online 2010)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1439-0469.2010.00586.x).
- Jarvis et al. use "core landbirds (Telluraves)", Australaves, Afroaves, Accipitrimorphae, Coraciimorphae and a further name "Coraciornithia" (Fig. 3 labels) and redefine Falconiformes to exclude eagles and New World vultures ("now in Accipitrimorphae") — [Jarvis et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451). Suh et al. 2015 cite Jarvis 2014 for Accipitrimorphae ("eagles/New World vulture clade") and Coraciimorphae ("sensu stricto without mousebird"), and cite Australaves to ref. [25] — [Suh et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1002224).
- Hieraves ("uniting Accipitriformes (hawks) and Strigiformes (owls)") and Aquaterraves ("waterbirds and relatives") — [Wu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2319696121). Elementaves ("terrestrial, aquatic and aerial niches, corresponding to the classical elements") — [Stiller et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1).
- Sangster et al. reviewed 10 phylogenomic data sets, defined clades congruent in ≥4 of them, and provided PhyloCode definitions for 25 names (23 minimum-crown-clade), introducing Dinocrypturi, Pteroclimesites, Musophagotides, Phaethoquornithes and Pelecanes — [Sangster et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.avrs.2022.100027) (abstract only). Gatesy & Springer note that "Afroaves, Musophagotides, and Coraciimorphae + Strigiformes" are not resolved in their consensus but were featured in Reddy et al.'s summaries — [Gatesy & Springer 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/genes13071167).

### Inferences
- When the Portuguese report uses "Afroaves" it should say the name refers to a clade whose monophyly is contested (not recovered by Prum 2015, Houde 2019 combined indel, Wu 2024, ROADIES 2025, or wASTRAL on B10K data), whereas Telluraves, Australaves, Eufalconimorphae and Psittacopasserae are well corroborated.
- "Accipitrimorphae" (Cathartidae + Accipitriformes s.s.) and "Accipitriformes" (with Cathartidae included, as in B10K/Stiller Fig. 1a, which lists no separate Cathartiformes) are used for the same clade in different classifications — inference from the figure labels.

### Gaps
- Original authorship and date of Accipitrimorphae, Coraciimorphae and Coraciornithia: UNVERIFIED.
- Whether Telluraves, Afroaves, Australaves, Eufalconimorphae, Psittacopasserae, Accipitrimorphae, Coraciimorphae or Cavitaves are among Sangster et al.'s 25 PhyloCode-defined names: UNVERIFIED (the preprint PDF available was image-only and the publisher page was blocked).
- Braun & Kimball 2021 attach reference [26] (Mayr 2011) to both "Telluraves" and "Australaves" in one passage, contradicting Yuri et al. 2013's own statement; this may be a citation slip or an indexing error on my side — flag for checking.
