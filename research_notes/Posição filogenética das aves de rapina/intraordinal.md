# Internal phylogenies and current classification of the raptor lineages (Cathartidae, Accipitriformes, Falconidae, Strigiformes)

Scope: relationships *within* each raptor lineage (families, subfamilies, key genera), the data behind them, conflicts, and resulting taxonomy, as of 17 Oct 2026. Inter-ordinal placement (Telluraves, Australaves/Afroaves) is touched only where it bears on Cathartidae vs Accipitriformes.

Source-quality legend used below: **[primary]** = read from the paper (abstract via PubMed, or article page); **[primary-page]** = article full-text page read through a fetch tool that summarises (numbers checked where possible, but wording is the tool's paraphrase); **[checklist]** = checklist file or committee document read directly; **[secondary]** = Wikipedia, search-engine summary, or website that cites the primary work; **UNVERIFIED** = could not be confirmed against a primary source in this session.

## Q1. Accipitriformes at family level: Cathartidae vs Accipitriformes, and the order Sagittariidae / Pandionidae / Accipitridae

### Takeaway
The best-supported arrangement is Cathartidae (Cathartiformes) as sister to Accipitriformes s.s. In Accipitriformes, Sagittariidae (Secretarybird) is sister to Pandionidae + Accipitridae: ((Sagittariidae, (Pandionidae, Accipitridae)), Cathartidae). The densest dated tree (Catanach et al. 2024) puts the Cathartiformes/Accipitriformes split at ~61.3 Mya, the Sagittariidae split at ~60.9 Mya, and the Pandionidae/Accipitridae split at ~50.8 Mya. AviList keeps Cathartiformes as a separate order placed next to Accipitriformes. Some genome studies instead treat Cathartidae as a family inside Accipitriformes; that is a difference of rank, not of topology.

### Cited Findings
- Catanach et al. 2024, UCE phylogeny of Accipitridae. Cathartiformes is sister to Accipitriformes. Outgroups were taxa from Pandionidae, Sagittariidae and Cathartidae. The authors quote: "the family Sagitariidae was sister to the rest of Accipitriformes, and the family Pandionidae was sister to the rest of Accipitridae" [primary-page] — [Catanach, Halley & Pirro 2024, Biol J Linn Soc, DOI 10.1093/biolinnean/blae028](https://doi.org/10.1093/biolinnean/blae028)
- Same study, BEAST dates with the topology held fixed: Cathartiformes + Accipitriformes 61.3 Mya; Sagittariidae split 60.9 Mya; Pandionidae vs Accipitridae 50.8 Mya; Elaninae vs rest of Accipitridae 45.3 Mya [primary-page] — [DOI 10.1093/biolinnean/blae028](https://doi.org/10.1093/biolinnean/blae028)
- Mitogenome phylogeny (Aquila fasciata and Buteo lagopus mitogenomes plus Accipitriformes data) "supported the robust monophyly of Accipitriformes, and Cathartidae was basal to the balance of the order". Here Cathartidae is treated as part of Accipitriformes s.l. [primary] — [Jiang et al. 2015, PLoS ONE, PMID 26295156, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0136297](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0136297)
- The Harpy Eagle chromosome-scale genome (1.35 Gb, N50 58.1 Mb, BUSCO 99.7%) shows "major chromosome-level rearrangements at the basal Accipitriformes genome, in contrast to a conserved ancient genome architecture" in Cathartidae and Galloanserae. This is a karyotypic/synteny character separating Accipitriformes s.s. from Cathartidae [primary] — [Canesin et al. 2024, Sci Rep, PMID 39261501, DOI 10.1038/s41598-024-70305-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-70305-w)
- 1995 cyt-b studies found that New World vultures are not close relatives of Accipitridae vultures. Wink (1995): carrion-feeding evolved at least three times (Cathartidae; Neophron–Gypaetus; Gyps–Aegypius). Cathartidae "share distant ancestry with storks but a close relationship is unlikely" [primary] — [Wink 1995, Z Naturforsch C, PMID 8561830, DOI 10.1515/znc-1995-11-1220](https://doi.org/10.1515/znc-1995-11-1220). Seibold & Helbig (1995): "the data support the contention that New World vultures are not birds of prey, but phylogenetic information was insufficient to identify whether they are closer to storks (Ciconiidae) or to Accipitriformes" [primary] — [PMID 8577858, DOI 10.1098/rstb.1995.0150](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.1995.0150). These results were later overturned by genome-scale data (see the next items and the AviList order sequence).
- B10K family-level genomes (363 species, 218 families, intergenic regions, coalescent methods). Main tree: "grouped Accipitriformes and Strigiformes as the sister to the remaining Afroaves". The concatenated tree put Accipitriformes alone as sister to the remaining Afroaves. Quartet frequencies for the two topologies were "statistically indistinguishable (35 versus 34.6%, χ2 test, P Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted = 0.130)". The owls + hawks clade appeared only when ≥138 Passeriformes were sampled [primary; full text via Europe PMC PMC11111414] — [Stiller et al. 2024, Nature 629:851–860, PMID 38560995, DOI 10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1)
- AviList v2025/v2025b order sequence (read from the checklist file): "... Apodiformes > Strigiformes > Cathartiformes > Accipitriformes > Coliiformes > ... > Cariamiformes > Falconiformes > Psittaciformes > Passeriformes". Accipitriformes = 3 families, 252 species, 76 genera (Sagittariidae 1 sp., Pandionidae 1 sp., Accipitridae 250 spp., 74 genera). Within the order the sequence is Sagittariidae, then Pandionidae, then Accipitridae [checklist] — [AviList Core Team 2026, AviList v2025b, DOI 10.2173/avilist.v2025b](https://doi.org/10.2173/avilist.v2025b); [AviList v2025b page](https://www.avilist.org/checklist/v2025b/)
- AOS/NACC: Falconidae is not closely related to Accipitridae. Large genetic studies place it "more closely related to the parrots (Psittaciformes) and songbirds (Passeriformes) than to the rest of the diurnal raptors (e.g., Hackett et al. 2008, Jarvis et al. 2014, Prum et al. 2015)" [checklist] — [AOS N&MA Proposals 2025-C, p. 2](https://americanornithology.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/2025-C.pdf)

### Inferences
- Statement suitable for the final report: Cathartidae (Cathartiformes) is the sister group of Accipitriformes s.s., from which it diverged ~61 Mya [Catanach 2024 dating]. Sagittariidae diverged next, almost immediately (~61 Mya). Pandionidae is sister to Accipitridae, with that split ~51 Mya. Accipitrimorphae (Cathartiformes + Accipitriformes) and Strigiformes may form a clade, but that node is near a polytomy in the genome-scale data (Stiller 2024).
- Ranking Cathartidae as an order (AviList, AOS) or as a family within Accipitriformes (B10K-style, Jiang 2015) is a convention. Both describe the same sister-group topology.

### Gaps
- The Stiller et al. 2024 text contains no explicit statement on Cathartidae. A keyword search for "Cathart" found no hits, so B10K appears to fold Cathartidae into "Accipitriformes" (UNVERIFIED). I found no node support value specifically for Cathartidae + Accipitriformes from a genome-scale study in this session.
- I could not read the Catanach et al. 2024 node-support figure for the outgroup nodes (Sagittariidae, Pandionidae). The AOS proposal says only 5 nodes in the whole tree lacked 100% bootstrap.

## Q2. Accipitridae: subfamilies, branching order, polyphyly of Old World vultures, and the break-up of *Accipiter*

### Takeaway
Catanach et al. 2024 is the current reference tree: UCEs from 134 genomes plus legacy markers, 237 species, ~90% of the family. Its backbone, almost all at 100% ultrafast bootstrap, is:

Elaninae → (Gypaetinae [with *Polyboroides* sister to the rest] + Perninae) → (Circaetinae + Aegypiinae) → Aquilinae → Harpiinae → Lophospizinae → [Accipitrinae s.l. + (Harpaginae + Buteoninae)]

Accipitrinae s.l. includes Melieracini, Kaupifalcini, Aerospizini, *Erythrotriorchis*, *Accipiter* s.s., *Astur*, and *Megatriorchis* + *Circus*. Buteoninae comprises Milvini and Buteonini.

Old World vultures are polyphyletic: Gypaetinae (*Gypaetus*, *Neophron*, *Gypohierax*) sits near the honey-buzzards, while Aegypiinae is sister to the snake-eagles. Earlier mtDNA + nuclear work had already shown that *Accipiter* is not monophyletic. Catanach et al. 2024 dismantled the genus into *Lophospiza*, *Aerospiza*, *Tachyspiza*, *Accipiter* s.s. and *Astur*, with *Microspizias* already separated in 2021. WGAC, Clements, IOC, AviList and AOS (66th Supplement, 2025) have all adopted the split.

### Cited Findings
**Historical molecular framework**
- Lerner & Mindell 2005 used ND2 (1047 bp), cyt-b (1041 bp) and β-fibrinogen intron 7 (1074 bp), sampling "representatives of all 14 Accipitridae subfamilies". They supported the monophyly of sea eagles (Haliaeetinae) and booted eagles (Aquilinae). Harpiinae, Circaetinae and Old World vultures were "found to be non-monophyletic". *Polyboroides typus* and *Geranospiza caerulescens* are "not … close relatives", a case of convergent evolution in limb morphology [primary] — [Lerner & Mindell 2005, MPE, PMID 15925523, DOI 10.1016/j.ympev.2005.04.010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ympev.2005.04.010)
- Griffiths et al. 2007 used the RAG-1 exon and recognised eight major lineages named at tribe/subtribe/infratribe ranks (Elanini, Gypaetina, Pernina, Circaetina, Gypina, Harpiita, Aquilita, Accipitrita) [secondary: search-engine summary; DOI verified on Crossref] — [Griffiths, Barrowclough, Groth & Mertz 2007, J Avian Biol 38:587–602, DOI 10.1111/j.0908-8857.2007.03971.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.0908-8857.2007.03971.x). A SACC proposal reports weak RAG-1 support for *Busarellus* grouping with *Butastur* and *Ictinia* [secondary] — [SACC Proposal 461](https://www.museum.lsu.edu/~Remsen/SACCprop461.htm)
- Mindell, Fuchs & Johnson 2018 (book chapter; DOI verified on Crossref) is cited by AOS for these findings: the *Elanus*/*Gampsonyx*/*Chelictinia* clade is sister to the rest of Accipitridae, and *A. trivirgatus* is sister to Accipitrinae + Buteoninae [checklist] — [Mindell et al. 2018, in *Birds of Prey*, Springer, pp. 3–32, DOI 10.1007/978-3-319-73745-4_1](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-73745-4_1); [AOS 2025-C](https://americanornithology.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/2025-C.pdf). A search snippet says Mindell et al. 2018 recognised 8 subfamilies, compared with 14 in Lerner & Mindell 2005, and used a non-monophyletic "transitory Accipitrinae" (UNVERIFIED; not checked against the chapter).
- Old World vulture polyphyly with cyt-b: the Aegypiinae core (*Gyps*, *Necrosyrtes*, *Aegypius*, *Torgos*, *Trigonoceps*, *Sarcogyps*) "shares a more recent common ancestor with several non-vulture genera … (Buteo, Aquila, Haliaeetus, Circaetus) than it does with … Gypaetus barbatus and Neophron percnopterus", which "seem to be each other's sister species and are closer to Pernis". The authors proposed the subfamily Gypaetinae for *Gypaetus* + *Neophron* [primary] — [Seibold & Helbig 1995, PMID 8577858, DOI 10.1098/rstb.1995.0150](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.1995.0150)

**Catanach et al. 2024 (the current reference tree)**
- Data, from the published version: UCEs from whole-genome sequencing of 134 species (2,360 UCE loci) plus legacy mt/nuclear markers. The time-calibrated tree has 237 extant or recently extinct species (~90% of recognised species) and 33.59% missing data in the full alignment. The text reports 226 of 249 (plus 2 extinct) in one place and "225 of 249" in the conclusion. The bioRxiv preprint (10.1101/2023.07.13.548898) gave 120 WGS species and 236 total. Methods: IQ-TREE v1.6 with GTR+G per UCE and 1000 ultrafast bootstraps; legacy-only taxa placed with RAxML-NG v1.1.0 using `--tree-constraint` on the UCE backbone; BEAST v1.7.5 dating on the fixed topology. No coalescent (ASTRAL) species tree is reported [primary-page] — [DOI 10.1093/biolinnean/blae028](https://doi.org/10.1093/biolinnean/blae028); preprint metadata [primary] — [bioRxiv 10.1101/2023.07.13.548898](https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.07.13.548898)
- Support: "Across their well-supported phylogeny, where only 5 nodes did not receive 100% bootstrap support, the genus Accipiter was found to form 5 distinct clades" [checklist] — [AOS 2025-C, p. 3](https://americanornithology.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/2025-C.pdf)
- Backbone order, as quoted from the Results [primary-page]: "The most basal split within Accipitridae separated the monophyletic subfamily Elaninae". Then Gypaetinae + Perninae "formed the sister group of the remainder". Then Circaetinae + Aegypiinae (reciprocally monophyletic). Then Aquilinae. Then Harpiinae, "sister to the Buteoninae + Accipitrinae clade". *Lophospiza trivirgatus* "occupies a long branch that is sister to the diverse Buteoninae + Accipitrinae clade" (bootstrap = 100). Finally, "The final two major clades were reciprocally monophyletic": Accipitrinae s.l. and Harpaginae + Buteoninae — [DOI 10.1093/biolinnean/blae028](https://doi.org/10.1093/biolinnean/blae028)
- Gypaetinae: "Our results support a monophyletic Gypaetinae only if Polyboroides is excluded". *Polyboroides* is sister to the rest of the Gypaetinae + Perninae clade, and the remaining gypaetines are sister to *Gypohierax angolensis*. No separate Polyboroidinae is recognised [primary-page] — [DOI 10.1093/biolinnean/blae028](https://doi.org/10.1093/biolinnean/blae028)
- New family-group names and placements [primary-page]:
  - **Lophospizinae** (new subfamily): *L. trivirgatus*, *L. griseiceps*.
  - **Harpaginae**: *Harpagus* + *Microspizias*, sister to Buteoninae. The text says "we recognize the genera Microspizias and Harpagus as members of the subfamily Harpaginae" (authorship of the name UNVERIFIED).
  - New tribes in Accipitrinae: **Melieracini** (*Melierax*, *Micronisus*, *Urotriorchis*), **Kaupifalcini** (*Kaupifalco*), **Aerospizini** (*Aerospiza* + *Tachyspiza*).
  - Buteoninae tribes: "Milvini (Milvus, Haliastur, and Haliaeetus) and Buteonini". *Butastur* and *Ictinia* are in Buteonini; *Geranospiza* is sister to *Busarellus* + *Rostrhamus* + *Helicolestes*.
  - *Helicolestes* and *Rostrhamus* are sister (~10.8 Mya); both are kept as monotypic genera.
  - *Macheiramphus* (Bat Hawk) is placed in Harpiinae with *Harpyopsis*, *Harpia* and *Morphnus*.
  - *Megatriorchis* is sister to *Circus* (BS 100).

  Source: [DOI 10.1093/biolinnean/blae028](https://doi.org/10.1093/biolinnean/blae028)
- Selected dates (Mya) [primary-page]: subfamily-level clades 25.1–33; Circaetinae crown 20.9; Aquilinae crown 16; Aegypiinae main split 12.3; Harpiinae splits 23.1 and 17.2; *Aerospiza*/*Tachyspiza* 17.9; *Erythrotriorchis* split 20.8; *Megatriorchis*/*Circus* 15.9; *Circus* crown 9.1; *Gyps* crown 2.7 — [DOI 10.1093/biolinnean/blae028](https://doi.org/10.1093/biolinnean/blae028)
- The Harpy Eagle genome is independently consistent with an Oligocene origin of the subfamilies. A burst of solo-LTRs/CR1 at ~31–33 MYA overlaps "the split of the ancestor to all Harpiinae from other Accipitridae subfamilies". An unknown-TE burst at ~13–22 MYA coincides with the split of *Harpia* from other Harpiinae [primary] — [Canesin et al. 2024, DOI 10.1038/s41598-024-70305-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-70305-w)
- 2026 single-genome papers that agree with the subfamily placements:
  - Philippine Eagle: "Phylogenetic analysis placed P. jefferyi within the subfamily Circaetinae" [primary] — [BMC Genomics 2026, PMID 42010461, DOI 10.1186/s12864-026-12859-9](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12864-026-12859-9)
  - Steppe Eagle: a UCE phylogenomic analysis "robustly placed [it] within Aquilinae". Species names are stripped from the PubMed abstract [primary] — [ZooKeys 1281, 2026, PMID 42291627, DOI 10.3897/zookeys.1281.158566](https://doi.org/10.3897/zookeys.1281.158566)

**Non-monophyly of *Accipiter* and the resulting genera** (AOS proposal summarising Catanach et al. 2024 [checklist] — [AOS 2025-C-1, pp. 1–5](https://americanornithology.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/2025-C.pdf))
- Background: "*Accipiter* was until recently the largest genus in Accipitridae, with 46-50 species". The five "Accipiter" clades "are not each other's closest relatives, and instead are successively sister, with other smaller genera (i.e., Circus, Megatriorchis, Erythrotriorchis, Kaupifalco, Melierax, Urotriorchis, and Micronisus) interspersed".
- *Lophospiza* Kaup 1844 (trivirgatus, griseiceps): "sister to a large clade that included all of subfamilies Accipitrinae and Buteoninae with strong support", also recovered by Mindell et al. 2018. *Griseiceps* was not sampled.
- *Aerospiza* Roberts 1922 (tachiro [incl. toussenelii], castanilius) and *Tachyspiza* Kaup 1844 (27 spp: badius, butleri, brevipes, soloensis, francesiae, trinotatus, hiogaster, novaehollandiae, fasciatus, melanochlamys, albogularis, haplochrous, rufitorques, henicogrammus, luteoschistaceus, imitator, poliocephalus, princeps, erythropus, minullus, gularis, nanus, virgatus, erythrauchen, cirrocephalus, brachyurus, rhodogaster) are sister clades. Together they are sister to the remaining "Accipiter" + *Erythrotriorchis* + *Megatriorchis* + *Circus*.
- *Accipiter* s.s. (nisus = type, madagascariensis, ovampensis, rufiventris, striatus, poliogaster). *Poliogaster* is "provisional": the only data were COI, and Mindell et al. 2018 had it sister to *Megatriorchis*, without strong support. *Dinospizias* Cabanis 1874 is available for it.
- *Astur* Lacépède 1799 (cooperii, gundlachi, bicolor, chilensis, melanoleucus, henstii, gentilis, atricapillus, meyerianus) is sister to *Megatriorchis* + *Circus*.
- *Circus* is retained even though it is nested in the former *Accipiter*. The linear sequence is changed so that *Circus* follows *Astur atricapillus*.
- *Microspizias* (superciliosus, collaris) was erected earlier by Sangster et al. 2021 [Crossref-verified DOI] — [Sangster et al. 2021, Vertebrate Zoology 71:419–424, DOI 10.3897/vz.71.e67501](https://doi.org/10.3897/vz.71.e67501)
- Adoption: "In 2024, WGAC voted to break Accipiter into 5 genera, following the recommendations of Catanach et al. (2024); this was followed by Clements et al. (2024) and the IOC World Bird List (Gill et al. 2025)" [checklist] — [AOS 2025-C](https://americanornithology.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/2025-C.pdf). AOS 66th Supplement (2025) adopted *Tachyspiza soloensis* and *Astur cooperii*/*gundlachi*/*bicolor*/*gentilis*/*atricapillus*. It moved *Circus* after *Astur atricapillus*, stating "Circus is sister to extralimital species Megatriorchis doriae" and "Circus + Megatriorchis are sister to Astur", and added *Accipiter nisus* to the main list [primary-page] — [Chesser et al. 2025, Ornithology 142(3) ukaf015, DOI 10.1093/ornithology/ukaf015](https://doi.org/10.1093/ornithology/ukaf015)

**Other generic changes now in AviList, driven by the same data** [checklist] — [AviList v2025b, DOI 10.2173/avilist.v2025b](https://doi.org/10.2173/avilist.v2025b)
- *Haliaeetus* is split. Northern *Haliaeetus* = leucocephalus, albicilla, leucoryphus, pelagicus. Southern *Icthyophaga* = leucogaster, sanfordi, vocifer, vociferoides, humilis, ichthyaetus. Based on Mindell et al. 2018 (mostly mtDNA) and "also supported by genomic DNA data (Catanach et al. 2024)".
- *Dryotriorchis spectabilis* is moved into *Circaetus* because Catanach et al. 2024 "recovered Circaetus as paraphyletic". AviList notes a three-genus alternative, if a name becomes available for the *C. cinerascens* + *C. fasciolatus* clade.
- *Microspizias*: AviList's decision text says "Although placed as sister to Kaupifalco, support for this relationship is unclear" (citing Mindell et al. 2018). Catanach et al. 2024 instead places *Microspizias* sister to *Harpagus* (see Conflicts).
- AviList Accipitridae linear sequence (250 spp., 74 genera), which mirrors the Catanach topology: Gampsonyx, Chelictinia, Elanus | Polyboroides, Gypohierax, Gypaetus, Neophron | Eutriorchis, Leptodon, Chondrohierax, Aviceda, Pernis, Elanoides, Hamirostra, Lophoictinia, Henicopernis | Sarcogyps, Trigonoceps, Aegypius, Torgos, Necrosyrtes, Gyps | Spilornis, Pithecophaga, Terathopius, Circaetus | Stephanoaetus, Nisaetus, Spizaetus, Lophotriorchis, Polemaetus, Lophaetus, Ictinaetus, Clanga, Hieraaetus, Aquila | Harpyopsis, Macheiramphus, Harpia, Morphnus | Lophospiza | Micronisus, Urotriorchis, Melierax, Kaupifalco, Aerospiza (2), Tachyspiza (27), Erythrotriorchis (2), Accipiter (6), Astur (9), Megatriorchis, Circus (16) | Harpagus, Microspizias | Haliastur, Milvus, Haliaeetus (4), Icthyophaga (6) | Butastur, Ictinia, Geranospiza, Busarellus, Helicolestes, Rostrhamus, Morphnarchus, Cryptoleucopteryx, Buteogallus, Rupornis, Parabuteo, Pseudastur, Geranoaetus, Leucopternis, Bermuteo, Buteo (27). The group breaks (|) are my annotation by subfamily/tribe; AviList itself has no subfamily rank.

### Inferences
- The best-supported Accipitridae topology, for the final report: (Elaninae, ((Gypaetinae incl. *Polyboroides*, Perninae), ((Circaetinae, Aegypiinae), (Aquilinae, (Harpiinae, (Lophospizinae, (Accipitrinae s.l., (Harpaginae, Buteoninae[Milvini + Buteonini])))))))).
- Old World vultures arose at least twice within Accipitridae. Gypaetinae branches near the base, with Perninae; Aegypiinae is sister to Circaetinae. Together with Cathartidae, this means three independent origins of obligate scavenging among the "vultures".
- Classic "kites" are polyphyletic. Elanine kites are basal; *Elanoides* and the Australian *Hamirostra*/*Lophoictinia* sit in Perninae; *Milvus*/*Haliastur* are in Milvini; *Ictinia*, *Rostrhamus* and *Helicolestes* are in Buteonini.
- The old 14-subfamily scheme no longer fits. Harpiinae is redefined around *Harpia*/*Morphnus*/*Harpyopsis*/*Macheiramphus*; *Pithecophaga* is in Circaetinae. "Milvinae" and "Haliaeetinae" are reduced to the tribe Milvini inside Buteoninae. Accipitrinae is expanded to absorb *Circus* (former Circinae), *Melierax*, *Micronisus*, *Urotriorchis* and *Kaupifalco*.
- Only the deep backbone rests on genome-scale data. Placements of the 103 legacy-only species, e.g. *A. poliogaster* (COI only), are constrained to the UCE backbone and are less secure.

### Gaps
- Conflict, unresolved: *Microspizias* is sister to *Kaupifalco* in Mindell et al. 2018 (support "unclear" per AviList) but sister to *Harpagus* in Catanach et al. 2024. The Catanach support value for Harpaginae was not readable in this session.
- I could not access exact bootstrap values for each backbone node beyond "only 5 nodes did not receive 100%". Catanach et al. 2024 ran no coalescent/ASTRAL or concordance-factor analysis, so gene-tree discordance at the short Oligocene internodes is unquantified.
- I found no 2025–2026 genome-scale study of the whole family that tests the Catanach backbone. The 2026 Philippine Eagle and Steppe Eagle genomes address single species only.
- A correction to Catanach et al. 2024 (blae051) is mentioned in a search summary, but its content was not checked (UNVERIFIED).
- I did not verify Griffiths et al. 2007 support values or the full Mindell et al. 2018 subfamily list against the original texts (paywalled).

## Q3. Cathartidae internal relationships

### Takeaway
Only one multilocus study samples all seven species. It finds two clades: (*Coragyps* + *Cathartes*) and (*Sarcoramphus*, *Gymnogyps*, *Vultur*). The crown split is ~14 Ma; the stem goes back ~69 Ma. Only *Coragyps* sister to a monophyletic *Cathartes* is robust across analyses. The arrangement inside the condor/king-vulture clade is unresolved, and no genome-scale intra-family tree was found.

### Cited Findings
- Johnson, Brown, Fuchs & Mindell 2016. Data: 5 nuclear loci (4060 bp) + 2 mitochondrial loci (2165 bp). Analyses: a fossil-calibrated gene tree with 27 outgroup taxa and a coalescent species tree with 2 outgroups, plus 4 more nuclear loci (2578 bp) in the species-tree analysis. Stem origin "∼69 million years ago (Ma; 74.5-64.9 credible interval)"; basal crown split "∼14Ma (17.1-11.1 credible interval)". Two clades: "(1) Black Vulture (Coragyps atratus) together with the three Cathartes species … and (2) King Vulture (Sarcoramphus papa), California (Gymnogyps californianus) and Andean (Vultur gryphus) Condors". "Support for taxon relationships within the two basal clades were inconsistent between analyses with the exception of Black Vulture sister to a monophyletic Cathartes clade". The additional loci increased support for a yellow-headed vulture clade (*C. burrovianus* + *C. melambrotus*) [primary] — [Johnson et al. 2016, MPE 105:193–199, PMID 27601346, DOI 10.1016/j.ympev.2016.08.025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ympev.2016.08.025)
- Ancestral-area reconstruction (BioGeoBEARS, BBM-MCMC) on that phylogeny puts the family's ancestral area in South America, with later dispersal back into North America. The species names are stripped in the PubMed abstract [primary] — [Cortés-Díaz et al. 2023, Animals 13:3175, PMID 37893899, DOI 10.3390/ani13203175](https://doi.org/10.3390/ani13203175)
- Cathartid genome architecture is conserved (ancestral-like), unlike the rearranged Accipitridae karyotype [primary] — [Canesin et al. 2024, DOI 10.1038/s41598-024-70305-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-70305-w)
- AviList: Cathartidae = 7 species, 5 genera, in the sequence Gymnogyps, Sarcoramphus, Vultur, Coragyps, Cathartes (3). No decision summaries flag a generic change [checklist] — [AviList v2025b, DOI 10.2173/avilist.v2025b](https://doi.org/10.2173/avilist.v2025b)

### Inferences
- For the report: Cathartidae = ((*Coragyps*, *Cathartes*), (*Sarcoramphus*, *Gymnogyps*, *Vultur*)). Treat the second clade as a soft polytomy. A recent (~14 Ma) crown on a very long (~69 Ma) stem implies the extant diversity is a small surviving remnant. The AviList sequence (Gymnogyps → Sarcoramphus → Vultur) does not make a pairing within the condor clade explicit.

### Gaps
- I found no UCE or whole-genome phylogeny of all seven cathartids, and no support values for any *Gymnogyps*/*Vultur*/*Sarcoramphus* pairing (the Johnson et al. 2016 full text was not accessible).

## Q4. Falconidae: Herpetotherinae, Polyborinae (incl. *Spiziapteryx*), Falconinae

### Takeaway
Three subfamilies are well supported: Herpetotherinae (*Herpetotheres* + *Micrastur*), Polyborinae/Caracarinae (*Spiziapteryx*, *Caracara*, *Ibycter*, *Daptrius* s.l.) and Falconinae (*Polihierax*, *Microhierax*, *Neohierax*, *Falco*). Herpetotherinae is sister to the other two. The key datasets are RAG-1 (Griffiths 2004) and 8 loci for all 64 species (Fuchs et al. 2015); there is no family-wide genome-scale study. Two generic changes resulted:
- *Milvago* is polyphyletic, so AviList/Clements lumped *Milvago* and *Phalcoboenus* into *Daptrius* (7 spp.).
- *Polihierax* is non-monophyletic, so *P. insignis* moved to *Neohierax*.

### Cited Findings
- Griffiths et al. 2004 sequenced "2800+ bp of the RAG-1 exon for representatives of all the currently recognized genera". RAG-1 agreed with syringeal morphology but "differed from the mitochondrial results with regard to monophyly of the genus Micrastur", which the authors attributed to cyt-b "heterogeneity in base composition". RAG-1 "identify more nodes with high bootstrap support indices than do either morphology or mitochondrial sequences" [primary] — [Griffiths et al. 2004, MPE, PMID 15186800, DOI 10.1016/j.ympev.2003.11.019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ympev.2003.11.019)
- Fuchs, Chen, Johnson & Mindell 2011 (*Micrastur*): basal split within the genus ~7 Myr; all speciation within the last 2.5–3.6 Myr [primary] — [PMID 21621621, DOI 10.1016/j.ympev.2011.05.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ympev.2011.05.008)
- Fuchs, Johnson & Mindell 2012 (caracaras): mtDNA 2308 bp + nDNA 5008 bp, all caracaras. "Clearly, Milvago as traditionally defined is not monophyletic". *M. chimango* is sister to *Phalcoboenus*, with "weak or no support" in the separate mt/nuclear analyses but "much stronger" support when concatenated. *Daptrius ater* is sister to *M. chimachima* in all analyses. The authors offered 4 options and recommended option 3 (move only *chimango* to *Phalcoboenus*) [secondary: SACC proposal by Remsen] — [SACC Proposal 561](https://www.museum.lsu.edu/~Remsen/SACCprop561.htm); article DOI verified on Crossref: [Fuchs et al. 2012, Ibis 154:520–532, DOI 10.1111/j.1474-919X.2012.01222.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1474-919X.2012.01222.x)
- Fuchs, Johnson & Mindell 2015 analysed "eight independent loci for 1-7 individuals from each of the 64 currently recognized Falconidae species" with two fossil calibrations. They found "similar diversification ages in the Early to Middle Miocene for the three traditional subfamilies, Herpetotherinae, Polyborinae and Falconinae". Falconinae divergences began ~16 mya and *Falco* (~60% of falconid species) ~7.5 mya. A significant diversification-rate increase at the base of *Falco* is correlated with C4 grassland expansion [primary] — [Fuchs et al. 2015, MPE 82:166–182, PMID 25256056, DOI 10.1016/j.ympev.2014.08.010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ympev.2014.08.010). A secondary source quotes 95% intervals of ~22.3–34.2 Mya (Falconidae), 12.6–19.3 (Falconinae) and 5.0–7.7 (*Falco*) (UNVERIFIED against the paper).
- Subfamily branching order: Herpetotherinae is the earliest-diverging lineage. Falconinae and Polyborinae split from it ~30.2 Mya and from each other ~20 Mya (Wikipedia citing Fuchs et al. 2015) [secondary] — [Wikipedia: Falconinae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falconinae); [Wikipedia: Falconidae, cladogram based on Fuchs et al. 2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falconidae). A secondary site reports a genome-scale dataset (198 species; 8,351 exons, 2,516 introns, 3,769 UCEs) placing "Herpetotherinae at the base, followed by … Polyborini and the Falconini" (dataset identity and date UNVERIFIED) — [ResearchGate: Phylogeny of Falconidae and phylogeography of Peregrine Falcons](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331118392_Phylogeny_of_Falconidae_and_phylogeography_of_Peregrine_Falcons)
- *Spiziapteryx* (Spot-winged Falconet) is in Polyborinae in the Fuchs et al. 2015-based cladogram [secondary] — [Wikipedia: Falconidae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falconidae). AviList places it first among the caracaras [checklist, below].
- AviList decisions [checklist] — [AviList v2025b, DOI 10.2173/avilist.v2025b](https://doi.org/10.2173/avilist.v2025b):
  - *Daptrius*: "Genetic data indicate that Milvago is not monophyletic (Fuchs et al. 2012a, 2015), with taxon chimango identified as the sister lineage to Phalcoboenus … chimango, the four species of Phalcoboenus, and M. chimachima, are folded into a single genus, Daptrius, consistent with evidence for a recent radiation in this group".
  - *Neohierax insignis*: "transferred to the genus Neohierax based on morphology of the syrinx (Griffiths 1994) and a multi-gene dataset (Fuchs et al. 2015); both indicate that the traditional genus Polihierax is not monophyletic".
- AviList Falconidae: 65 spp., 10 genera, in the sequence Herpetotheres (1), Micrastur (7) | Spiziapteryx (1), Caracara (2), Ibycter (1), Daptrius (7) | Polihierax (1), Microhierax (5), Neohierax (1), Falco (39) [checklist] — [AviList v2025b](https://doi.org/10.2173/avilist.v2025b)
- Within *Falco*, a 2023 genome study is intrageneric only: Altai falcons are polyphyletic within eastern Sakers, plus findings on the origin of Gyrfalcons [primary, title only] — [Sci Rep 2023, PMID 37853004, DOI 10.1038/s41598-023-44534-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-44534-4)

### Inferences
- For the report: Falconidae = (Herpetotherinae [*Herpetotheres*, *Micrastur*], (Polyborinae [*Spiziapteryx*, (*Caracara*, *Ibycter*, *Daptrius* s.l.)], Falconinae [*Polihierax*, *Microhierax* + *Neohierax*, *Falco*])). The arrangement of the caracara genera and of *Microhierax*/*Neohierax* is my reading of the AviList sequence plus the decision texts, not a verified tree.
- The subfamilies are old (Oligocene–early Miocene). The bulk of species richness (*Falco*) is a young Late Miocene–Pliocene radiation.

### Gaps
- I found no UCE or whole-genome phylogeny sampling all falconid genera (2015–2026). Fuchs et al. 2015 (8 loci) remains the reference.
- The exact position of *Spiziapteryx* within Polyborinae, the relationships among *Caracara*/*Ibycter*/*Daptrius*, and the sister group of *Neohierax* (*Microhierax*?) were not verified against Fuchs et al. 2015 support values (paywalled).
- The family-group name for the caracara clade (Polyborinae vs Caracarinae; Gregory 2024 cited by bird-phylogeny.de) is UNVERIFIED.

## Q5. Strigiformes: Tytonidae vs Strigidae, *Phodilus*, and Strigidae subfamilies

### Takeaway
Tytonidae (*Tyto* + *Phodilus*) and Strigidae are reciprocally monophyletic. *Phodilus* is the sister of *Tyto* within Tytonidae, with a common ancestor ~28 Mya (Oligocene, Australasia). Strigidae has three main lineages:
- Ieraglaucinae (= Ninoxinae): *Ninox*, *Uroglaux*; *Margarobyas* only in mtDNA.
- Surniinae: *Athene*, *Glaucidium*, *Aegolius*, *Surnia*, *Micrathene*, *Xenoglaux*, *Taenioptynx*.
- Striginae: Otini, Asionini, Bubonini, Megascopini, Pulsatrigini, Strigini.

In Salter et al. 2020 (UCEs, 4k loci) the nuclear data group Ieraglaucinae with Surniinae (clade A), as sister to Striginae (clade B). mtDNA instead places Ieraglaucinae sister to all other strigids. "Asioninae" is nested in Striginae. Eight genera were non-monophyletic, which drove extensive generic lumping in AviList/IOC.

### Cited Findings
- Wink, El-Sayed, Sauer-Gürth & Gonzalez 2009: cyt-b + RAG-1 for 97 owl taxa. Strigiformes = Tytonidae + Strigidae; Strigidae fall into three subfamilies, Striginae, Surniinae and Ninoxinae. Asioninae "does not form a distinct subfamily, but clusters within the Striginae" with 94% bootstrap support [secondary: search summary of the Ardea article; DOI verified on Crossref] — [Wink et al. 2009, Ardea 97:581–591, DOI 10.5253/078.097.0425](https://doi.org/10.5253/078.097.0425)
- Uva et al. 2018 (Tytonidae): 16 of 17 *Tyto* species and 1 of 3 *Phodilus*; 7 mitochondrial + 2 nuclear markers. "The owl family Tytonidae comprises two genera: Phodilus … and the ubiquitous Tyto". "The most recent common ancestor between the Tytonidae genera Phodilus and Tyto was probably from the Oligocene (ca. 28 mya) of Australasia". *Tyto alba* s.l. = *T. furcata* + *T. alba* + *T. javanica*, with a Late Miocene (~6 mya) origin of the barn-owl group and "six major Pleistocene radiations" [primary] — [Uva et al. 2018, MPE, PMID 29535030, DOI 10.1016/j.ympev.2018.03.013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ympev.2018.03.013)
- An earlier COI study: the Old World *alba*-clade and New World *furcata*-clade are "genetically equidistant", with 5.33–6.20% inter-clade COI divergence [primary] — [Nijman & Aliabadian 2013, Zool Sci, PMID 24199866, DOI 10.2108/zsj.30.1005](https://doi.org/10.2108/zsj.30.1005)
- Salter et al. 2020 (UCEs) [primary-page] — [Salter et al. 2020, The Auk 137(1) ukz070, DOI 10.1093/auk/ukz070](https://doi.org/10.1093/auk/ukz070); abstract via [LSU repository](https://repository.lsu.edu/biosci_pubs/495):
  - Data and methods: 43 strigid species from all but one genus, *Tyto alba* as the barn-owl representative, and outgroups *Apaloderma*, *Falco* and *Haliaeetus*. 75%-complete matrix of 4,253 UCE loci; the paper also reports 4,325 and 4,235 in different places. 2,034,468 characters, 99,241 parsimony-informative sites. ExaML and ExaBayes gave identical topologies; ASTRAL-III and SVDQuartets were also run. The mt dataset is 13 protein-coding genes for 55 taxa (11,366 characters). Nodes are "100% bootstrap support / Bayesian posterior probability (BPP) unless otherwise labeled".
  - Results: barn owls and typical owls are reciprocally monophyletic. Nuclear data give two strigid clades. Clade A = Ninoxini (*Uroglaux* sister to *Sceloglaux* + *Ninox*) plus Surniinae, with *Margarobyas* sister to Surniinae. Clade B = Striginae: Otini sister to the rest, then Asionini (*Ptilopsis* + *Asio* s.l.), then Bubonini, Megascopini, Pulsatrigini and Strigini.
  - mtDNA instead recovers a Clade C (Ieraglaucinae: *Margarobyas*, *Sceloglaux*, *Ninox*) sister to A + B. The name Ieraglaucinae has priority over Ninoxinae.
  - Unstable taxa: *Uroglaux* is in clade A when concatenated but sister to A + B in the coalescent analysis. *Jubula* is sister to Bubonini (concatenated) or to the rest of Striginae (SVDQuartets). The authors "place more weight on the concatenated and coalescent topologies".
  - Abstract: "the genera Athene, Otus, Asio, Megascops, Bubo, and Strix are paraphyletic" and "Ninox and Glaucidium are polyphyletic". *Ninox jacquinoti* nests in *Athene*; *Glaucidium brodiei* is sister to *Micrathene* + *Xenoglaux*. Up to 177 of 194 species could be affected.
  - Recommendations: *N. jacquinoti* → *Athene*; *Sceloglaux* → *Ninox*; *Pyrroglaux* and *Mimizuku* → *Otus*; *Nesasio* and *Pseudoscops* → *Asio*; *Scotopelia* and *Ketupa* → *Bubo*; *Ciccaba* → *Strix*; *Megascops nudipes* → *Psiloscops*; *G. brodiei* → *Taenioptynx*.
- Wood et al. 2016 (dated 2016 on Crossref, often cited as 2017): a high-coverage mitogenome of the extinct Laughing Owl places *Sceloglaux* inside *Ninox*. The authors recommend *Ninox albifacies* [secondary summary; DOI verified on Crossref] — [Wood et al. 2016, Zool J Linn Soc 179:907–918, DOI 10.1111/zoj.12483](https://doi.org/10.1111/zoj.12483)
- Osteology, 35 characters and 7 *Asio* species: the result "supports the previously proposed synonymization of Pseudoscops and Nesasio into Asio". It conflicts with molecular data over the *A. otus*/*grammicus*/*clamator* relationships (*grammicus* + *clamator*: BS 100, Bremer 12) [primary] — [Posso & Salomão 2025, Zootaxa 5584, PMID 40174068, DOI 10.11646/zootaxa.5584.3.5](https://doi.org/10.11646/zootaxa.5584.3.5)
- AviList Strigiformes = 2 families, 245 spp., 25 genera [checklist] — [AviList v2025b, DOI 10.2173/avilist.v2025b](https://doi.org/10.2173/avilist.v2025b):
  - Tytonidae: *Phodilus* (2), *Tyto* (15). "Taxon prigoginei is placed in Tyto … The heart-shaped facial disc of prigoginei is Tyto-like, suggesting similarities to Phodilus are superficial or convergent. Genetic data … are desirable".
  - Strigidae: 228 spp., 23 genera, in the sequence Uroglaux, Margarobyas, Ninox (37) | Taenioptynx (2), Xenoglaux, Micrathene, Aegolius (5), Athene (9), Surnia, Glaucidium (26) | Otus (58), Ptilopsis (2), Asio (9), Jubula, Bubo (10), Scotopelia (3), Ketupa (12), Psiloscops, Gymnasio, Megascops (22), Lophostrix, Pulsatrix (3), Strix (21).
  - Decisions: *Sceloglaux* → *Ninox albifacies* (Salter et al. 2020; Wood et al. 2017); *Ninox jacquinoti* → *Athene* and split into four species; *Pyrroglaux podargina* → *Otus podarginus*; *Nesasio solomonensis* and *Pseudoscops grammicus* → *Asio*; *Ciccaba* → *Strix* ("denser taxon sampling, including the type species of Ciccaba, is needed"); *nudipes* → monotypic *Gymnasio* (to avoid a paraphyletic *Megascops*; nuclear, mt and genomic data).
  - *Ketupa* is expanded to 12 species (blakistoni, ketupu, zeylonensis, flavipes plus poensis, lactea, nipalensis, sumatrana from genetics, and shelleyi, coromanda, leucosticta, philippensis provisionally from morphology). AviList recognises "a reduced Bubo (Old and New World taxa); an expanded Ketupa (tropical Afro-Asian taxa); and … Scotopelia, recovered as the sister clade to Ketupa based on genomic data (Salter et al. 2020), or embedded within it based on mitochondrial DNA (Wink & Sauer-Gürth 2021)".
  - *T. alba* s.l. is split into *T. furcata*, *T. javanica* and *T. alba* (Aliabadian et al. 2016; Uva et al. 2018; Robb 2015).
- Stiller et al. 2024: Strigiformes + Accipitriformes is recovered as a clade in the coalescent main tree, but with near-equal quartet support for the alternative (see Q1) [primary] — [DOI 10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1)

### Inferences
- For the report: Strigiformes = (Tytonidae [(*Phodilus*, *Tyto*)], Strigidae [(Ieraglaucinae?, Surniinae), Striginae]). The nuclear UCE topology (Ieraglaucinae + Surniinae) is preferred by its authors over the mitochondrial alternative (Ieraglaucinae sister to all). The Tytonidae/Strigidae split is far older than the Tyto–Phodilus divergence (~28 Mya).
- AviList departs from Salter et al.'s recommendations in three places. It keeps *Ketupa*/*Scotopelia* separate from *Bubo*. It places *nudipes* in *Gymnasio* rather than *Psiloscops*. Its sequence places *Margarobyas* next to *Uroglaux*/*Ninox*, which matches mtDNA Clade C rather than the nuclear placement as sister to Surniinae.
- "Asioninae" should not be used as a subfamily (Wink 2009, Salter 2020).

### Gaps
- I found no strigiform phylogeny from 2021–2026 that is genome-scale and covers all genera. Salter et al. 2020 (43 species, UCEs) remains the most comprehensive nuclear phylogenomic study; the next search hits were single-species genomes and mitogenomes.
- *Xenoglaux*: the abstract says "all but one genus" were sampled, yet the fetched text places *G. brodiei* sister to *Micrathene* + *Xenoglaux*. An AOS blog says later work placed *Xenoglaux* "within the Athene clade" ([AOS blog](https://americanornithology.org/unraveling-the-mystery-of-the-long-whiskered-owlet/)). The exact placement of *Xenoglaux* is UNVERIFIED.
- Only one of three *Phodilus* species was sampled in Uva et al. 2018. *Tyto prigoginei* (formerly *Phodilus*) has no genetic data.
- Wink et al. 2009 support values beyond the quoted 94% (Asioninae in Striginae) were not accessible. Wink & Sauer-Gürth 2021 (cited by AviList) was not checked.

## Q6. Current raptor classification in AviList and IOC, and what changed vs older checklists

### Takeaway
AviList v2025 (June 2025) and v2025b (June 2026; no taxonomic changes) give:
- Cathartiformes: 7 spp.
- Accipitriformes: Sagittariidae, Pandionidae, Accipitridae; 252 spp.
- Strigiformes: Tytonidae + Strigidae; 245 spp.
- Falconiformes: 65 spp.

These are four separate orders, and Falconiformes sits far from the others, next to parrots and passerines. AviList has no subfamily rank, so the subfamily structure exists only in the linear sequence. Compared with older checklists (e.g., the 46–50-species *Accipiter* of HBW/H&M 4, IOC ≤14.x), the main changes are in genera:
- *Accipiter* split five ways, plus *Microspizias*.
- *Haliaeetus*/*Icthyophaga* split; *Dryotriorchis* sunk into *Circaetus*.
- *Daptrius* expanded; *Neohierax* resurrected.
- Owl genera lumped (*Sceloglaux*, *Pyrroglaux*, *Nesasio*, *Pseudoscops*, *Ciccaba*); *Taenioptynx* and *Gymnasio* created; *Ketupa* expanded.
- *Tyto alba* split into three species.

IOC's site lists v15.2 as current. IOC adopted the *Accipiter* split in v15.1.

### Cited Findings
- AviList v2025b: "AviList Core Team. 2026. AviList: The Global Avian Checklist, v2025b. https://doi.org/10.2173/avilist.v2025b". The v2025b changes are minor: alignment with eBird/Clements v2025 and BirdLife v10, the proposal-number format, and errata [checklist] — [AviList v2025b page](https://www.avilist.org/checklist/v2025b/); DOI verified on Crossref (AviList v2025b, Christidis, Rheindt, Iliff, Lepage…). The spreadsheet's "Changes" sheet states that "the taxonomic concepts, scientific names, English names, a[nd] taxonomic sequence of AviList v2025b have not changed at all from v2025—both version recognize exactly the same 11,131 species concepts and 19,879 subspecies concepts" [checklist] — [AviList v2025b short xlsx](https://www.avilist.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/AviList-v2025b-10Jun2026-short.xlsx)

### Inferences
- The global checklists (AviList, which IOC/Clements/BirdLife feed into) and the regional AOS checklist now agree on the *Accipiter* split. The AOS treats *poliogaster* in *Accipiter* tentatively, with *Dinospizias* proposed as an alternative.
- Because AviList has no subfamilies, any subfamily or tribe names in the final report (Lophospizinae, Harpaginae, Melieracini, Ieraglaucinae, Herpetotherinae…) must be attributed to the phylogenetic papers (Catanach 2024, Salter 2020, Fuchs 2015), not to the checklists.

- The AviList companion paper (Biodiversity and Conservation, July 2025) DOI was not retrieved. Only the checklist DOI 10.2173/avilist.v2025b was verified.
