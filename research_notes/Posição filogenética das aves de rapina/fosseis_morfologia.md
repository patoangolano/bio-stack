## 1. Key fossil taxa: age, unit/locality, specimen, published affinity

### Takeaway
Only a handful of Paleogene fossils have been placed by explicit analyses. These are Masillaraptoridae (stem Falconiformes), Antarctoboenus (stem Falconidae), Messelasturidae/Halcyornithidae (stem Pan-Psittaciformes or stem Psittacopasseres, weakly supported), and Bathornis (stem Cariamiformes, outside Cariamidae + Phorusrhacidae). Most early owls, accipitrids, ospreys, secretarybirds and "cathartids" are known from isolated leg bones and have never been included in a formal phylogenetic analysis. The oldest owls (Paleocene, about 61–57.5 Ma) predate the oldest securely identified accipitrids (late Eocene–early Oligocene) by about 25 million years.

### Inferences
- The raptor-relevant Paleogene record is concentrated in three Lagerstätten-type sources: Messel (about 48–47 Ma), the London Clay at Walton-on-the-Naze and the Green River Formation (both early Eocene). It is also biased toward the Northern Hemisphere. That bias weakens biogeographic inferences such as Ericson's Gondwanan scenario.
- Owls are the earliest raptorial crown-order lineage with a fossil record (Paleocene). Yet no Paleogene owl has been placed by a formal analysis. A tip-dating or placement study must treat Ogygoptynx and Berruornis as leg-bone-only taxa with huge missing data.
- The co-occurrence of Masillaraptoridae, Messelasturidae, Halcyornithidae, Strigogyps and Palaeoglaux at Messel makes Messel the key assemblage for testing raptorial character evolution near the base of Australaves.

### Gaps
- Li et al. 2025 (*M. buchheimi*): abstract, phylogenetic result, any CT data and the age in Ma could not be read (Deep Blue HTTP 403).
- Ages in Ma for Walton-on-the-Naze (London Clay) and the Green River beds that yielded *Tynskya* and *M. buchheimi* were not found in a primary source here.
- No primary 2019–2026 reassessment of *Diatropornis* / *Phasmagyps* (true Cathartiformes or not) was found. Mayr's 2022 chapter is the likely source — [doi:10.1007/978-3-030-87645-6_8](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-87645-6_8) — but its text was not accessible.

- Sagittariid genus names, Bathornithidae temporal range beyond *B. grallator*, and *Taubatornis* primary citation details are UNVERIFIED.

## 2. How morphology-based analyses placed raptors, and why they conflict with genomics

### Takeaway
Morphological matrices repeatedly united the diurnal raptors with owls. Mayr & Clarke (2003) recovered owls as sister to Falconidae + Accipitridae. Livezey & Zusi (2007) recovered Strigiformes sister to Falconiformes, with Cathartidae as the basal falconiform. Genome-scale data instead scatter these lineages across Telluraves: falcons sit next to parrots and passerines (Australaves), while Accipitriformes, Cathartiformes and owls sit in Afroaves. This implies extensive convergence in raptorial hindlimb and beak characters. Fossils show the same convergence directly: a Paleogene owl with an accipitrid-like foot, and an Oligocene accipitrid with an *Accipiter*-like build acquired convergently.

### Cited Findings
- **Mayr & Clarke 2003** (43 neornithine higher taxa, 148 osteological and soft-tissue characters): the traditional "Falconiformes" (Cathartidae, Sagittariidae, Accipitridae, Falconidae) was not monophyletic, because "Strigiformes (owls) are placed as the sister taxon of (Falconidae + Accipitridae)" — [Cladistics 19:527–553, doi:10.1111/j.1096-0031.2003.tb00387.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1096-0031.2003.tb00387.x).
- **Livezey & Zusi 2006/2007** (150 neornithine taxa plus 35 outgroups, 2954 morphological characters, 97 MPTs). Findings include "(v) Strigiformes and Falconiformes are sister-groups; (vi) Cathartidae is the sister-group of the remaining Falconiformes" and "(ix) traditional Caprimulgiformes are monophyletic and the sister-group of the Apodiformes" — [ZJLS 149:1–95, doi:10.1111/j.1096-3642.2006.00293.x, PMID 18784798, PMCID PMC2517308](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1096-3642.2006.00293.x).
- **Mayr 2008** reanalysed the Livezey & Zusi characters: "Avian higher-level phylogeny: well-supported clades and what we can learn from a phylogenetic analysis of 2954 morphological characters" (JZSER 46:63–72). Lloyd's transcription has 188 taxa × 2954 characters — [Lloyd NEXUS](https://www.graemetlloyd.com/nexus/Mayr_2008c.nex).
- **Mayr, Manegold & Johansson 2003**, "Monophyletic groups within 'higher land birds' – comparison of morphological and molecular data" (JZSER 41:233–248). The matrix (29 taxa × 89 characters) includes Falconidae, Accipitridae and Strigidae/Tytonidae — [Lloyd NEXUS](https://www.graemetlloyd.com/nexus/Mayr_etal_2003a.nex).
- **Syringeal morphology**: Griffiths 1994, "Monophyly of the Falconiformes based on syringeal morphology" (Auk 111:787–805), and Griffiths 1999, "Phylogeny of the Falconidae inferred from molecular and morphological data" (Auk 116:116–130). Both are listed but have no NEXUS file — [Lloyd listing](https://www.graemetlloyd.com/matrbird.html).
- **Molecular counter-evidence**:
  - Hackett et al. 2008 (19 loci, 169 species) found parrots + passerines and stated that "some diurnal birds evolved from nocturnal ancestors" — [Science, doi:10.1126/science.1157704, PMID 18583609](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1157704).
  - Jarvis et al. 2014 (48 genomes) named Passerea/Columbea and noted "massive protein-coding sequence convergence" at deep nodes — [Science 346:1320–1331, doi:10.1126/science.1253451, PMID 25504713](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451).
  - Prum et al. 2015 (259 loci, 198 species) — [doi:10.1038/nature15697](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature15697).
  - Kuhl et al. 2021 (3′-UTR, ~90% of families) — [doi:10.1093/molbev/msaa191](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msaa191).
  - Stiller et al. 2024 (363 genomes, 218 families) — [Nature 629:851–860, doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1).
  - Wu et al. 2024 (124 species, 25,460 loci; Telluraves vs "Aquaterraves") — [PNAS 121:e2319696121, doi:10.1073/pnas.2319696121](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2319696121).
- McClure & Johnson summarise the molecular picture: "falcons are as closely related to songbirds and parrots as they are to seriemas". They add that Stiller et al. 2024 and Wu et al. 2024 show "owls are more closely related to a clade that includes Accipitriformes and Cathartiformes than previously proposed" — [McClure & Johnson 2025, State of the World's Raptors ch. 2, doi:10.4080/swr.weoq8259](https://doi.org/10.4080/swr.weoq8259).
- Ericson's family-level biogeography groups falcons + seriemas as a South American radiation. Owls, diurnal raptors except falcons, and New World vultures belong to an African radiation. "Many cases of convergently evolved adaptations may be revealed" — [Ericson 2012, J. Biogeogr. 39:813–824, doi:10.1111/j.1365-2699.2011.02650.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2699.2011.02650.x). Earlier dated Neoaves tree: [Ericson et al. 2006, Biol. Lett., doi:10.1098/rsbl.2006.0523](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsbl.2006.0523).
- **Convergent raptorial characters**:
  - Mayr & Kitchener's 2022 character list for Masillaraptoridae includes "Upper beak with sharply hooked tip", "Upper beak, tomial grooves", "Upper beak dorsoventrally very deep and mediolaterally compressed" and coracoid/tarsometatarsal characters — [Figshare supplement, Mayr_and_Kitchener_Supplementary_Data.docx](https://tandf.figshare.com/articles/dataset/New_fossils_from_the_London_Clay_show_that_the_Eocene_Masillaraptoridae_are_stem_group_representatives_of_falcons_Aves_Falconiformes_/20267628).
  - McClure & Johnson argue that "morphological criteria often posited to define raptors (keen eyes, sharp talons, and a hooked beak) are poor delimiters" — [doi:10.4080/swr.weoq8259](https://doi.org/10.4080/swr.weoq8259).
  - An Oxford thesis found that hypotarsus shape is a significant indicator of raptoriality. Raptorial birds have "a deep and wide monosulcate hypotarsus" — [ORA uuid:90912ef0… (author/year UNVERIFIED; page returned 403; text from search snippet)](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:90912ef0-a777-4dbc-b0d9-7ff42d0c15b7).
- **Fossil demonstrations of convergence**:
  - *Primoptynx*: an owl with accipitrid-like ungual proportions — [doi:10.1080/02724634.2020.1769116](https://doi.org/10.1080/02724634.2020.1769116).
  - *Aviraptor*: "convergently acquired an Accipiter-like morphology" — [doi:10.1007/s00114-020-01703-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00114-020-01703-z).
  - Messelasturids share derived characters "with only distantly related extant taxa (Accipitriformes, Strigiformes, Falconiformes, and Psittaciformes)"; unconstrained analyses were unresolved, while a molecular-scaffold analysis put them sister to Psittaciformes + Passeriformes — [Mayr 2021, doi:10.1007/s12542-020-00541-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12542-020-00541-8).
  - Semizygodactyly evolved independently in Coliiformes, Leptosomiformes and Strigiformes — [Ksepka et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1700188114).
- **Total-evidence / tip-dating**: "Tip-dating and the origin of Telluraves" — [Crouch, Ramanauskas & Igić 2019, MPE 131:55–63, doi:10.1016/j.ympev.2018.10.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ympev.2018.10.006). Its precursor abstract title was "Total-evidence analyses support a Cretaceous origin of Telluraves" — [GSA 2016, doi:10.1130/abs/2016am-276740](https://doi.org/10.1130/abs/2016am-276740). Combined analysis of Pan-Psittaciformes — [Ksepka et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1666/10-108.1). Morphology-plus-molecular-scaffold analyses — [Mayr 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12542-020-00541-8); [Mayr & Kitchener 2023](https://www.schweizerbart.de/papers/njgpa/detail/307/102756/The_Vastanavidae_and_Messelasturidae_Aves_from_the).

### Inferences
- The owl + diurnal raptor clade in Mayr & Clarke (2003) and Livezey & Zusi (2007) is best explained as hindlimb and beak convergence linked to predation. Raptorial fossils with mosaic features (messelasturids, masillaraptorids, *Primoptynx*) are therefore expected to be "attracted" to the wrong crown orders in unconstrained parsimony analyses. That is why Mayr's recent papers report a molecular-scaffold result alongside the unconstrained one.
- A computational placement analysis should use a molecular backbone constraint (e.g., Stiller 2024 or Wu 2024 topology). It should also test sensitivity to removing or downweighting the obvious raptorial characters (hooked beak, enlarged claws, hypotarsus), since these drive the conflict.
- Livezey & Zusi (2007) put Caprimulgiformes with Apodiformes, not with owls. So the "owls + nightjars" grouping is not what the largest morphology matrix found.

### Gaps
- No tool-verified source was found for the historical owls + nightjars (Strigiformes + Caprimulgiformes) hypothesis (e.g., Sibley & Ahlquist). Treat it as UNVERIFIED here.
- The Oxford hypotarsus thesis could not be opened (403), so its author, year, sample and methods are unknown.
- The abstract and analysis details of Crouch et al. 2019 were not retrieved, so it is unknown which raptor fossils were tip-dated.
- No dedicated 2019–2026 total-evidence analysis covering all raptorial orders and fossils was found.

## 3. Status of the "raptorial (apex-predator) ancestor of Telluraves/Australaves" hypothesis

### Takeaway
The hypothesis originated as a parsimony inference on genome-scale trees. Jarvis et al. 2014 inferred "the common ancestor of core landbirds to have been an apex predator". It was formalised in the McClure et al. 2019 raptor definition, and McClure & Johnson 2025 supported it with an ancestral-state reconstruction of vertivory. Fossil-based work by Mayr is more cautious. Carnivory may be plesiomorphic for Psittacopasserae or for Falconiformes + Cariamiformes, but homoplasy is extensive. A hindlimb-morphometric thesis found raptoriality evolved convergently in each extant group, and a herbivorous Eocene cariamiform (*Strigogyps*) complicates simple optimisation. Status as of Oct 2026: plausible and repeatedly cited, but not demonstrated. It is sensitive to topology (Afroaves monophyly, owl position) and to how "raptorial" is coded.

### Cited Findings
- Jarvis et al. 2014: "Among Passerea, we infer the common ancestor of core landbirds to have been an apex predator" — [doi:10.1126/science.1253451](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451); [Europe PMC PMC4405904](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?format=json&resultType=core&pageSize=1&query=DOI%3A%2210.1126%2Fscience.1253451%22).
- McClure et al. 2019 define raptors as species in orders that "evolved from a raptorial land bird lineage and in which most species maintained their raptorial lifestyle". The definition includes Accipitriformes, Strigiformes, Cathartiformes, Falconiformes and Cariamiformes (secondary summary) — [J. Raptor Res. 53:419–430, doi:10.3356/0892-1016-53.4.419](https://doi.org/10.3356/0892-1016-53.4.419).
- McClure & Johnson 2025:
  - Methods: EltonTraits diet data, consensus of 10,000 BirdTree trees on the Hackett backbone, Rphylopars `anc.recon()` and phytools stochastic mapping.
  - Vertivory (≥10% vertebrates in the diet) was lost a median of 1173.5 times and gained 1121 times, so it is labile.
  - It nonetheless "clustered relative to common ancestry" in Telluraves and in Phaethoquornithes, which "suggest[s] that the common ancestor of landbirds was vertivorous".
  - Text: "as previously suggested based on basal branching patterns (Jarvis et al. 2014, Prum et al. 2015), the common ancestor of all core landbirds possessed a raptorial lifestyle (Wu et al. 2021)".
  - Raptor criteria: (1) Telluraves, (2) order with a vertivorous common ancestor, (3) ≥50% vertivorous species.
  - Source: [doi:10.4080/swr.weoq8259](https://doi.org/10.4080/swr.weoq8259). Cited genomic study: [Wu et al. 2021, BMC Ecol. Evol. 21:162, doi:10.1186/s12862-021-01888-5](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12862-021-01888-5).
- McClure & Johnson on seriemas: "The case for seriemas being raptors is moderate, despite their extinct raptorial relatives". Jarvis et al. 2014 "listed the seriemas as birds of prey because they are predatory and closely related to falcons" — [doi:10.4080/swr.weoq8259](https://doi.org/10.4080/swr.weoq8259).
- Mayr & Kitchener 2023: "a carnivorous ecology may be plesiomorphic for the Psittacopasseres", because all Cariamiformes and Falconiformes are raptorial. They also stress substantial homoplasy and possible convergence between messelasturids and falconiforms — [doi:10.1127/njgpa/2023/1119](https://www.schweizerbart.de/papers/njgpa/detail/307/102756/The_Vastanavidae_and_Messelasturidae_Aves_from_the).
- Mayr & Kitchener 2022: falcon–phorusrhacid beak and tarsometatarsus similarities in masillaraptorids "may be plesiomorphic for a clade including falconiforms and cariamiforms" — [abstract](https://nms.iro.bl.uk/concern/articles/4cbcb3f1-bae1-479d-838e-ba6ea42e0fdd).
- The Oxford ORA thesis states that "all extant raptorial groups have convergently evolved raptoriality, contradicting existing hypotheses of a raptorial ancestry of Telluraves as a whole". It adds that this "does not rule out the occurrence of carnivory in the ancestor of Telluraves" (search snippet; author UNVERIFIED) — [ORA](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:90912ef0-a777-4dbc-b0d9-7ff42d0c15b7).
- Counterexample within Cariamiformes: *Strigogyps sapea* (Messel) was herbivorous — [Mayr & Richter 2011, doi:10.1007/s12542-010-0094-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12542-010-0094-5). Wikipedia's Telluraves article reports critics calling the raptorial-ancestor inference "biased towards the more well known, predatory representatives of the clade" (secondary) — [Wikipedia Telluraves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telluraves).
- Topology sensitivity: Wu et al. 2024 recovered a novel Telluraves vs Aquaterraves dichotomy — [doi:10.1073/pnas.2319696121](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2319696121). Stiller et al. 2024 report "a marked degree of discordance" and recalcitrant nodes — [doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1). Phylogenetic definitions of clade names (Telluraves, Australaves, etc.) — [Sangster et al. 2022, Avian Research 13:100027, doi:10.1016/j.avrs.2022.100027](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.avrs.2022.100027).
- Timing: Mayr argues that "essentially modern-type representatives of Telluraves … in the Cretaceous … is highly unlikely" — [Mayr 2014, Palaeontology 57:231–242, doi:10.1111/pala.12103](https://doi.org/10.1111/pala.12103). Total-evidence tip-dating argued for a Cretaceous Telluraves origin — [Crouch et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ympev.2018.10.006); [GSA abstract](https://doi.org/10.1130/abs/2016am-276740). Wu et al. 2024 date the start of modern bird diversification to the Late Cretaceous — [doi:10.1073/pnas.2319696121](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2319696121).

### Inferences
- The hypothesis rests mainly on node-optimisation of a binary "raptorial" or "vertivorous" ecology on extant-only trees. Adding fossil stem taxa with known diets could change the optimisation at the Australaves and Telluraves nodes. Candidates are raptorial messelasturids, masillaraptorids and *Danielsraptor*; the herbivorous *Strigogyps*; and non-raptorial stem parrots and stem passerines (Zygodactylidae). Doing this with the matrices in section 4 is an untested opportunity.
- The morphological convergence evidence (hypotarsus, *Primoptynx*, *Aviraptor*) argues against homology of the specific raptorial apparatus. It is compatible with a generally carnivorous ancestor. "Carnivorous ancestor" and "raptorial apparatus homologous" should be treated as separate hypotheses.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed 2019–2026 ancestral-state study was found that includes fossils and explicitly tests a raptorial Telluraves ancestor. McClure & Johnson 2025 is a book chapter using extant taxa only.
- The specific Mayr text critiquing Jarvis et al.'s apex-predator inference (likely in *Avian Evolution*, 2017, [doi:10.1002/9781119020677](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781119020677), or the 2022 *Paleogene Fossil Birds* chapter) was not accessible.
- No source was found that confirms the full text of McClure et al. 2019. The summary of its definition is secondary.

### Inferences
- A practical fossil-placement pipeline has three steps:
  1. Merge the Mayr 2011c (Messelasturidae/Halcyornithidae), Mayr & Kitchener 2022 (Masillaraptoridae) and Mayr 2016 (Cariamiformes) matrices. All are Mayr-authored with overlapping character concepts.
  2. Add owls and accipitriforms from Ksepka et al. 2017 or Mather 2021.
  3. Run constrained placement (e.g., IQ-TREE/RAxML-ng EPA or MrBayes with a fixed backbone) on Stiller 2024 or Wu 2024.
- Rescoring is needed for the Paleogene owls (Ogygoptynx, Berruornis, Protostrigidae, Primoptynx), Horusornis, Aviraptor and Antarctoboenus, because none of the inspected public matrices contains them.
- The Mayr matrices are small, with 40–110 characters and heavy missing data for fossils. Expect weak support. Bayesian Mk with a molecular backbone and character-partition sensitivity tests are preferable to unconstrained parsimony.
- CT-based endocast and inner-ear comparison of Paleogene raptors is currently impossible from open data. The only fossil raptor CT asset found (the *Argentavis* endocast) is restricted.

### Gaps
- Matrices were not found as downloadable files for:
  - Mayr & Kitchener 2023 (Messelasturidae/Vastanavidae), Mayr & Kitchener 2024 (Halcyornithidae), Mayr 2021 (Tynskya, "emended data matrix"), Mayr 2026 (halcyornithid);
  - Li et al. 2025 (*M. buchheimi*), Cenizo et al. 2016 (Antarctoboenus), Crouch et al. 2019;
  - Ksepka et al. 2011.
  - These likely sit in publisher supplements (Springer ESM, Schweizerbart, Elsevier) that were not accessible. All are UNVERIFIED.
- The MorphoBank API is blocked (HTTP 403) and pages render only with a browser. Character and taxon counts for P510 and P2212/P2809 were not read.
- MorphoSource `q=` searches match titles and taxonomy only loosely. A taxonomy-filtered or browser search could reveal more raptor scans (e.g., oVert Accipitriformes, Strigiformes). The counts above are minimums.
- Whether *M. buchheimi* (3D skull from the Green River Fm.) or *T. waltonensis* (3D London Clay bones) were micro-CT scanned, and whether those scans are public, is unknown.
